Less phone scrolling at family gatherings

On holidays you visit relatives, on weekends the whole family has lunch together, or you meet friends for dinner. You sit at the table and your hand is still on short videos and short dramas. An older relative asks you something twice before you hear it, and the meal ends with little real talk. Kids do the same at a relative's home. Once the adults start chatting, someone hands them a tablet or an old phone, and a few cousins spend the whole afternoon on games and videos. The site has a mealtime page for everyday meals at home, a weekends page for lying around at home, and a travel page for the road. This page covers the few hours at someone else's home or out with family and friends. Ripple only manages the phones and tablets it is installed on. It cannot manage relatives' phones, tablets or TVs, so that part needs a word between the adults beforehand. The first part below is for adults limiting their own phone, the second is for parents setting up the tablet or phone a child brings along. Android is at v0.1.14 and iPhone at v0.1.5, and the features differ, so both are covered.

Adults: less phone while you sit with family and friends

Start one-tap focus before you leave

A gathering usually lasts a few hours. Before you head out, tap one-tap focus on Android and pick a length that fits, for example 3 hours. Choose Lock only the apps I pick and select short video, short drama and games. Leave WeChat, calls, maps and ride apps unpicked, so finding the way and reaching people still work. See the one-tap focus page.

Set daily limits and open limits on short video and short drama

Over a holiday there may be a meal out every day, and starting focus each time is easy to forget. Give short video and short drama a daily limit in custom minutes, or 0 minutes to block them all day. Add how many times they can open and turn on the short wait before each open, so when the table goes quiet and your hand reaches for the phone, you pause first. See the short drama page and the open limit page.

Use a schedule if the gathering is at a fixed time

Some families have lunch together every Sunday. Add a Sunday noon schedule with only short video, short drama and games on the list. Those apps will not open during it and come back when it ends.

WeChat Channels and Moments can start with a few minutes

Photos get shared in the family group during the meal. On Android you can limit only Channels and Moments inside WeChat: start with a short allowance, then write a reason to extend, up to 3 times a day, counted toward the WeChat daily limit. In strict mode, extending needs the unlock code. WeChat chats, photo sharing and voice calls still work.

Turn on strict mode if you tend to switch rules off

If you get bored at the table and turn the rules off, turn on strict mode. Give an unlock code of at least 8 digits to family, and loosening rules then needs their code. An unlock code can succeed at most 5 times a day. Strict mode also turns on Anti-Uninstall. If you forget the code, you can request release after 72 hours. See the strict mode page.

Parents: a child on a tablet all afternoon at a relative's home

Set daily limits on the child's own tablet or old phone first

Before the visit, give games and short video a daily limit on the Android tablet or old phone the child uses. When time is up they stop opening, even with cousins around. A game that is especially hard to stop can be set to 0 minutes and opened up again at home. See the mobile games page.

Parents keep the unlock code

When the allowance runs out, kids often ask a grandparent or an aunt to unlock it. If only parents know the code, relatives cannot unlock it even if they want to. Turn on Anti-Uninstall on Android so the child cannot remove Ripple. For a firmer lock, turn on strict mode, which also turns on Anti-Uninstall.

Changing the clock does not help

Older kids may move the system clock forward to reset the allowance early. Ripple for Android counts time since the phone started, so changing the clock gives no extra time. See the clock change page.

Split screen, floating windows and picture-in-picture are blocked too

Some kids drag a video into a floating window or picture-in-picture and play at the same time. On Android, listed apps are blocked in split screen, floating windows and picture-in-picture, and that time counts toward today's usage. On OPPO a floating window is covered and muted, and the window itself still has to be closed by hand.

Talk to relatives about their devices

Ripple cannot manage relatives' phones, tablets or TVs. If the child plays on a cousin's phone, the adults need to agree on it beforehand. If the child uses an iPhone, v0.1.5 can block apps by schedule, block deleting apps, protect against clock changes and use an unlock code. Daily limits, 0 minutes, open limits, one-tap focus, strict mode and Channels allowance are Android only for now. See the parental control page.

Setup steps (Android v0.1.14)

Install or update to the latest version

Download the latest Android APK and turn on accessibility when asked. If you have an older build, update in Settings › About › Check for updates. On iPhone install v0.1.5, which needs iOS 16 or later.

Adult phone: one-tap focus before you leave, or daily limits and open limits on short video and short drama

Leave WeChat, calls, maps and ride apps off the list. Add a schedule if the gathering is at a fixed time.

Child's tablet or old phone: daily limits or 0 minutes on games and short video before the visit

Leave study apps and apps for reaching parents off the list.

Set unlock codes and Anti-Uninstall on the child's device

Give your own code to family. Parents keep the code for the child's device and do not share it with relatives. Turn on Anti-Uninstall on Android, with strict mode for a firmer lock. On iPhone turn on the block on deleting apps.

FAQ

I keep looking down at short video at family gatherings. What helps?

On Ripple for Android v0.1.14, start one-tap focus before you leave and lock only short video, short drama and games for the length of the gathering. If there is a meal out every day over the holiday, give those apps daily limits and open limits instead. WeChat chats, calls and maps still work. For a firmer rule, turn on strict mode and give the unlock code to family.

My child plays games on a tablet all afternoon at a relative's home. What can I set?

Before the visit, give games and short video a daily limit on the child's Android tablet or old phone, or set a game to 0 minutes. Parents keep the unlock code and turn on Anti-Uninstall. Ripple counts time since the phone started, so changing the clock does not help. Ripple cannot manage relatives' phones or tablets, so agree on that with the adults beforehand.

Can Ripple manage relatives' phones or TVs?

No. Ripple only manages apps on the Android phones, Android tablets and iPhones it is installed on. Other people's phones, tablets and TVs are outside it.

How is this different from the mealtime and weekends pages?

The mealtime page covers everyday meals at home, weekends covers lying around at home all day, and travel covers the road. This page covers the hours at a relative's home or out with family and friends. More answers are on the FAQ.

Does the iPhone version have these features?

iPhone v0.1.5 has schedules, the block on deleting apps, clock change protection and an unlock code. Daily limits, open limits, 0 minutes, one-tap focus, strict mode, Channels allowance and website blocking are Android only.

Does it cost money or need an account?

It is free with no ads and no account.
