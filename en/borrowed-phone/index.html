Kids keep borrowing your phone? Set it up before you hand it over

Many kids do not have a phone of their own yet, so they play on a parent's. You hand it over for one cartoon episode and get it back an hour of short videos later, sometimes with WeChat, a payment app or a shopping app opened along the way. Ripple runs on your own phone and its rules apply to the phone, whoever is holding it. This page first covers lending the phone to a child, then how the same rules help you with your own use. Ripple for Android is v0.1.20 and Ripple for iPhone is v0.1.5, which does much less, so the two are covered separately.

When you lend the phone to a child

Tap Quick Focus before you hand it over

On Android, tap start on the Today page and pick 30 or 45 minutes. By default only the apps on your allowed list can open, for example a cartoon app or a learning app, and it ends by itself when time is up. Ending early needs a wait first (a fixed 15 minutes) or the unlock code.

Lock only the apps a child should not touch

Quick Focus can also lock only the apps you pick. Lock WeChat, payment apps, shopping apps and work apps and leave the rest open, so a child cannot get into chats or payments. Calls, SMS, alarms and the camera always work.

A game the child plays is installed on your phone

Give that game a daily limit, for example 30 minutes a day. When it is used up the game stays closed until midnight. You can also set how many times a day it can be opened, or a pause of a few seconds before each open. Time in split screen, floating windows and picture-in-picture counts too.

The child tries to change the settings

With strict mode on, rules can only be loosened with the unlock code, and you keep the code. Strict mode turns on Anti-Uninstall, so Ripple cannot be removed. Turning the clock back does not help because Ripple counts time since boot.

See what was used when you get it back

Tap the Time used today card on the Today page to open the usage calendar, then tap today to see how long each app was used, how many times it was opened and how many times it was blocked. Records stay on the phone.

Very young kids who only need one app

Android has a built-in screen pinning (app pinning) setting that keeps the phone in the current app until it is unlocked. Where it is depends on the brand, usually under Security or More security settings. It works alongside Ripple.

If you use an iPhone

Ripple for iPhone v0.1.5 only blocks apps on a schedule, blocks deleting apps, protects the clock and uses an unlock code. It has no Quick Focus and no daily limits. For lending an iPhone, the built-in Guided Access fits better: turn it on in Accessibility settings, open the app the child will use and triple-click the side button. The phone stays in that app until a passcode is entered, and you can set a time limit. Ripple can add a fixed schedule on top, for example short video apps and games closed after dinner.

You use this phone too

Rules apply to the whole phone

Ripple cannot tell who is using the phone. If you give a game 30 minutes a day for the child, your own play counts too. The other way round, limits you set for your own short video apps also apply while the child has the phone.

Put limits on your own scrolling too

Give TikTok, Instagram or other feeds a daily limit or an open limit, and add a bedtime schedule. The same rules apply in your hands and in the child's. You can tap Quick Focus for yourself too, before a meeting or while helping with homework.

Who keeps the unlock code

If you only want limits for the child, keep the code yourself. If you want limits on yourself as well, give it to the other parent or a friend. The code can be used successfully at most 3 times a day; a wrong code or cancel does not count. A forgotten code can be requested and is lifted after 72 hours.

Your own iPhone

Create blocking schedules for work hours and bedtime, turn on block deleting apps and give the unlock code to someone at home. Daily limits and open limits are only on Android for now.

Setup steps

Install Ripple

On Android, download the latest APK from the website and turn on the accessibility service as the app guides you. On Xiaomi, OPPO, vivo, Huawei and Honor phones, also follow the keep alive guide for autostart and battery settings. On iPhone you need iOS 16 or later and Screen Time permission.

Fill in the allowed list

Put the apps a child may use on the Quick Focus allowed list, for example cartoons, picture books and learning apps. Calls and alarms always work and do not need to be added.

Set daily limits on games and short videos

Open the app in Daily limits and pick the minutes. Pick 0 minutes to keep it closed all day.

Turn on strict mode and Anti-Uninstall

The unlock code needs at least 8 characters. Remember it and do not save it on the phone.

Tap once before each hand over

Start Quick Focus before you hand the phone over and look at the usage calendar when you get it back. See Quick Focus and the usage calendar.

FAQ

Can Ripple tell whether my child or I am using the phone?

No. Rules apply to the whole phone. Start Quick Focus before lending it and it ends by itself; to take it back sooner, wait out the delay or enter the unlock code.

What if someone calls while the child has the phone?

Calls, SMS, alarms and the camera always work and are not affected by Quick Focus.

How long can Quick Focus run?

25, 45, 60 or 90 minutes or 2 hours, or a custom length from 15 minutes to 12 hours.

Can a child get more time by changing the clock?

No. Ripple counts time since boot, so changing the system clock does not end a focus session early or add daily time.

Does the iPhone app have Quick Focus?

No. Ripple for iPhone v0.1.5 has no Quick Focus, daily limits or usage calendar. When lending an iPhone, use the built-in Guided Access, and use Ripple for fixed blocking schedules.

Does it cost anything?

Ripple is free, with no ads, no in-app purchases and no account. More in the FAQ.
