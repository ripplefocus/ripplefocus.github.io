Ripple vs Opal and Freedom

Opal and Freedom are two of the best known app blockers. Opal started on iPhone and launched on Android in summer 2025, and it has more than 500,000 downloads on Google Play. Freedom has been around for years and runs on phones and computers, syncing blocks through one account. Both have free plans, and schedules and lock features need a subscription. This page says where they do more than Ripple and who Ripple fits. Adults setting limits for themselves come first, then parents setting up a child's phone. Ripple for Android is on v0.1.20 and Ripple for iPhone on v0.1.5. Details about Opal and Freedom come from their websites, help centers and Google Play listings in November 2026, so check their latest pages.

Adults limiting their own phone

What Opal does well

Focus sessions, schedules, daily time limits, a Pomodoro timer, weekly reports and streaks, in a very polished app. You can pick a level where breaks and leaving early are not allowed. It runs on iPhone, Mac and Android. The free plan has one rule. Pro is listed on Opal's site at $19.99 a month, $99.99 a year or $399 lifetime, with student discounts. Its help center says the Android app does not yet have feature parity with iPhone, and the Emergency Pass is not on Android yet.

What Freedom does well

It blocks apps, websites or the whole internet, and one account syncs blocks across phones, tablets, Mac, Windows and Chromebook, so it also covers distractions on your computer. With Locked Mode on, you cannot end a running session or remove items from its blocklist. The free plan only starts sessions right away, up to 2 hours each. Schedules, recurring sessions and Locked Mode need Premium, listed at $39.99 a year on Google Play.

Where Ripple for Android overlaps

Ripple for Android v0.1.20 also has schedules, daily limits, Quick Focus, a pause before opening, open limits and blocked websites. All of it is free, with no in-app purchases and no account.

Where Ripple for Android differs

You install it from the website, so it works without Google Play, including on phones sold in mainland China. It can close only WeChat Channels and Moments while chat keeps working. In strict mode, rules can only be loosened with an unlock code of at least 8 characters, used successfully at most 3 times a day, which you can give to a partner or friend. Anti-Uninstall turns on with it, and a forgotten code can be removed 72 hours after a request. A 0 minute limit keeps an app closed all day.

What Ripple cannot do

There is no desktop app and no sync between devices, so for computer distractions Freedom is the better fit. It has no streaks or weekly reports like Opal. Ripple for iPhone v0.1.5 only blocks by schedule, blocks deleting apps, protects the clock and uses an unlock code, so on iPhone Opal does a lot more.

Parents setting up a child's phone

First: Ripple has no remote management

Ripple's settings live on that phone, so you set them up on the child's phone directly. Opal lists parental control, and Freedom is mainly made for people limiting themselves. To check or change things from your own phone, use Google Family Link on Android or Family Sharing on iPhone.

The child's phone has no Google Play

Many Android phones sold in mainland China cannot install apps from Google Play. Ripple installs from an APK on its website and does not need an account for the child.

The child turns it off or uninstalls it

Turn on strict mode on the child's phone and keep the unlock code yourself. Anti-Uninstall turns on with it, and changing the phone clock does not help. To keep one game closed all day, give it 0 minutes.

The child needs WeChat to reach the family

Close only Channels and Moments. Chat, voice and video calls keep working. For homework time, a schedule can close games and short video apps together.

The child uses an iPhone or iPad

Ripple for iPhone can block by schedule on the child's device, for homework time and bedtime, with the unlock code kept by a parent. For daily limits or remote management, iPhone Screen Time with Family Sharing covers more.

How to choose

You want your phone and computer covered together

Choose Freedom. One account syncs to your computer, and Ripple is phone only.

You use an iPhone and are fine with a subscription

Opal has many more features on iPhone and a nicer design. If a few fixed blocking schedules with a code kept by family are enough, Ripple for iPhone is free.

You have an Android phone and do not want a subscription

Install Ripple for Android. It is free, downloads from the website and needs no Google services or account.

Rules keep getting switched off by you or your child

Use strict mode and Anti-Uninstall in Ripple for Android, and give the unlock code to someone else. See also vs one sec and ScreenZen.

FAQ

Is Ripple a free Opal alternative?

On Android, the basics such as schedules, daily limits and Quick Focus are free in Ripple. Opal adds streaks, weekly reports, a Mac app and a more polished design. On iPhone, Opal does far more than Ripple v0.1.5.

How is Freedom's Locked Mode different from Ripple's strict mode?

Freedom's Locked Mode stops you from ending a running session or removing blocked items. Ripple's strict mode asks for the unlock code to loosen any rule, turns on Anti-Uninstall, and the code can be kept by someone else.

Can Ripple manage my child's phone remotely?

No. Ripple has no remote management. Set it up on the child's phone.

Does Ripple collect data?

Settings stay on the phone and there is no account. Since v0.1.6 it counts once a day, anonymously, on first open, with only the platform, version, days since install, new install or update, app language and a random install ID made on the phone. No usage content is sent.

Does it cost anything?

Ripple is free, with no ads and no in-app purchases. More in the FAQ.
