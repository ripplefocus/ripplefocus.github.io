Ripple vs one sec and ScreenZen

one sec and ScreenZen both make you wait a few seconds before a distracting app opens, and both are popular. one sec has published research behind it and its free version covers one app, with Pro for more. ScreenZen is free and supported by donations, and it has a lot of features. Both are good apps, and in some areas, especially on iPhone, they do more than Ripple. This page lays out the differences honestly. Adults setting limits for themselves come first, then parents setting up a child's phone. Ripple for Android is on v0.1.18 and Ripple for iPhone on v0.1.5, and they have different features. Details about one sec and ScreenZen come from their store listings and websites in November 2026, so check their latest pages.

Adults limiting their own phone

What one sec does well

Before Instagram or TikTok opens, it shows a breathing animation. In a published study app openings dropped by 57% on average. After a set time it can step in again. It runs on iPhone and Android and has a desktop browser extension. The free version covers one app. More apps, schedules and block sessions need Pro, listed at $19.99 a year or $99.99 lifetime on the US App Store.

What ScreenZen does well

Everything is free. It adds a wait before an app opens and the wait can grow with each open. It can strictly block apps by time range or after a number of opens, lock its settings until a time you pick or until an accountability partner lets you in, and prevent uninstalling. It runs on iPhone, Android, Mac and Windows. If a pause before opening is what you want and your phone gets apps from Google Play or the App Store, ScreenZen is a very good choice.

Where Ripple for Android overlaps

Ripple for Android v0.1.18 also has a pause before opening (5 to 30 seconds, optionally 5 seconds longer each open up to 60), an open limit, daily limits and schedules. All of it is free with no in-app purchases.

Where Ripple for Android is different

The APK downloads straight from the website, so it does not need Google Play and installs on phones sold in mainland China. It can close just WeChat Channels and Moments while chat keeps working, and just YouTube Shorts or Instagram Reels. With strict mode on, the only way to loosen rules is the unlock code. It has at least 8 characters and works at most 3 times a day, so a partner or friend can keep it. Strict mode also turns on Anti-Uninstall, and a forgotten code can be removed by request after 72 hours. Time is counted since the phone started, so changing the clock does not help. There are also 0 minute limits (an app stays closed all day) and Quick Focus.

On iPhone, plainly

Ripple for iPhone v0.1.5 only blocks apps by schedule, blocks deleting apps, protects against clock changes and uses an unlock code. It has no pause before opening, daily limits or open limit. If you want a pause before opening on iPhone, one sec or ScreenZen will suit you better.

Parents setting up a child's phone

First: Ripple has no remote management

Ripple settings stay on the phone they are set on, so you set it up on your child's device. To check or change things from your own phone, use Google Family Link on Android or Family Sharing on iPhone. one sec has a family plan and ScreenZen has an accountability partner unlock, which work best for an older child who is on board.

A child who opens apps every few minutes

On your child's Android phone give a video app a 20 second pause and turn on the longer wait with each open. The screen shows how many times it was opened today. Give their favorite game 3 opens a day, and it stays closed once they are used.

A child who changes settings or uninstalls

Turn on strict mode and keep the unlock code yourself. Anti-Uninstall turns on with it, and changing the clock does not help. If a game should stay closed all day, give it 0 minutes.

A child who needs WeChat to reach the family

Close only WeChat Channels and Moments. Chat, voice and video calls keep working. The one sec and ScreenZen listings do not mention WeChat Channels.

A child on iPhone or iPad

Ripple for iPhone can block apps on the child's device by schedule, such as homework time and bedtime, with the parent holding the unlock code. For daily limits or remote management, built in Screen Time with Family Sharing does more.

How to choose

You want a pause before opening and your phone has Google Play or the App Store

Try ScreenZen first, since it is free. If you like the breathing pause and only need one app, the free version of one sec works.

Your phone has no Google Play, or the apps are Douyin, Kuaishou or WeChat Channels

Install Ripple on Android. It is free, downloads from the website and needs no Google services or account.

You or your child keep switching the rules off

Use Ripple for Android with strict mode and Anti-Uninstall, and give the unlock code to someone else.

You use an iPhone

For a pause before opening, pick one sec or ScreenZen. If you only need a few fixed schedules with a family member holding the code, Ripple for iPhone can do that. See also Ripple vs built in tools.

FAQ

Ripple and ScreenZen are both free. What is the difference?

On iPhone ScreenZen has many more features. On Android both have a pause before opening, open limits and schedules. Ripple adds installing without Google Play, closing only WeChat Channels and Moments, and a strict mode where only an unlock code someone else keeps can loosen rules.

Can I use Ripple with one sec or ScreenZen?

You can install them on the same phone. Do not set a pause for the same app in both, or you will wait twice.

Can Ripple manage my child's phone remotely?

No. There is no remote management. Set it up on the child's phone.

Does Ripple collect data?

Settings stay on the phone and there is no account. Since v0.1.6 it counts once a day, anonymously, on first open. The count carries only the platform, version, days since install, new install or update, app language and a random install ID made on the phone, with no usage content.

Does it cost anything?

Ripple is free, with no ads and no in-app purchases. See the FAQ.
