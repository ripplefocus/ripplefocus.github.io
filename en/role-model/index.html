Want your kids on their phone less? Start with your own phone

Many parents know this moment. You ask your child to put the tablet away, then sit on the sofa scrolling short videos, and your child says "you are on your phone too". Kids copy how adults use their phones more than they follow what adults say. What tends to hold up is adults and kids each having rules that start on the same day. The first part covers limits parents set on their own phone, the second part covers the kids, and how to line the two up. Android is on v0.1.20 and iPhone on v0.1.5, with different features, so they are described separately.

Parents: rules for your own phone first

Start with the hours your kids are home

From after school to bedtime is when your kids see you on the phone most. You can cover only those hours, for example a schedule from 18:30 to 21:30 on weekdays with short video, games and live streaming on the list. Calls and alarm still work, and WeChat or other chat apps left off the list keep working for messages.

A cap for the rest of the day

On Android give short video apps a daily limit in minutes, for example 40 minutes a day, after which they stay closed for the day. For apps you open many times a day, add a daily open limit and the pause before opening. See the open limit page.

Lock the phone while you are with them

When you help with homework or play together, Android Quick Focus locks the phone right away, for example for 30 minutes, and ends on its own.

Give the unlock code to your partner

With strict mode on, the unlock code is the only way to loosen rules. In strict mode the code is at least 8 characters with at most 3 successful uses a day, and your partner or a grandparent can keep it. Strict mode also turns on Anti-Uninstall. If you forget the code you can request removal, which takes effect after 72 hours.

Parent on iPhone

iPhone v0.1.5 blocks apps by schedule, for example short video and games from dinner until the kids are in bed. It can also block deleting apps, protect against clock changes and use an unlock code. It has no daily limits, open limits, pause before opening, Quick Focus, Complete Lock or strict mode.

Kids: rules that match yours

Show your child your rules first

Before setting up their phone, show your child the schedule and limits on yours and tell them you also stay off these apps in the evening. Kids accept rules more easily when they see adults keep them too.

Put their schedules next to yours

On your child's Android phone or tablet give games and short video apps a daily limit, for example 30 minutes on school days and 90 minutes at weekends. Homework and sleep hours become schedules, and they can sit in the same hours as yours so the whole family puts phones down together. See the homework page.

Pause one game for a while

A game you agreed to drop for now can be set to 0 minutes so it stays closed all day, and changed back on the agreed date.

Your child cannot change it

Turn on strict mode on their phone and keep the unlock code yourself. With Anti-Uninstall on, your child cannot delete Ripple. Android counts time since boot, so changing the system clock gives no extra time. For revision hours turn on Complete Lock, which leaves only essential system apps such as phone, messages and alarm, and the unlock code cannot end it. On Xiaomi, OPPO, vivo, Huawei and Honor phones follow the keep alive guide for auto start and battery settings.

Child on iPhone or iPad

iPhone v0.1.5 blocks apps by schedule, so the apps stay closed during homework and sleep hours. It can block deleting apps, protect against clock changes and use an unlock code, with no daily limits or open limits. See the parental control page.

Setup steps

Install Ripple on both phones

On Android download the latest APK and turn on the accessibility service as guided. On iPhone install v0.1.5, which needs iOS 16 or later.

Set up your own phone first

Add a schedule for the hours your kids are home, then give the apps you find hardest to put down a daily limit.

Then your child's

Use the limits and bedtimes you agreed on, line their schedules up with yours, and turn on strict mode and Anti-Uninstall.

Check together after a week

At the weekend look at who kept to it this week and adjust together if it feels too tight or too loose. In strict mode changing rules needs the unlock code.

FAQ

I want my kids on their phone less but I cannot stop scrolling myself. What can I do?

Set rules on your own phone first. In Ripple add a schedule for the hours your kids are home with short video and games on the list, and give those apps a daily limit. On Android turn on strict mode and let your partner keep the unlock code.

I need to answer work messages in the evening. Can I still use this?

Yes. Put only short video, games and live streaming on the list and leave WeChat, phone and email off it, so they keep working.

My child says "you are on your phone too". How do I answer?

Show your child the rules on your phone, agree on the same phone free hours, and keep them together. On their Android phone turn on strict mode and Anti-Uninstall and keep the unlock code yourself.

Can my child turn it off?

With strict mode on, loosening rules needs the unlock code and Anti-Uninstall is on, so your child cannot delete Ripple. Complete Lock on revision hours cannot be ended with the unlock code.

Does this work on iPhone?

iPhone v0.1.5 blocks selected apps by schedule, can block deleting apps, protects against clock changes and has an unlock code. It has no daily limits, open limits, Quick Focus, Complete Lock or strict mode.

Is it free? Do I need an account?

It is free with no ads and no account. More in the FAQ.
