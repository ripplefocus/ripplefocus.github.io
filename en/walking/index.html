Less scrolling while you walk

Walking from home to the station, from the bus stop to the office, or out for a walk after dinner, the phone stays in your hand and short videos keep coming. You look up and find you have passed your turn, or nearly walked into someone or a pole. Kids do the same on the way to and from school, watching videos or playing games as they walk, and parents worry most about them crossing the street without looking. The site also has a commute page for scrolling while seated on the subway or bus and a school hours page for class time. This page covers the time you spend on foot. Looking at a phone while walking takes your attention off the road, which is common sense, so this page lists no statistics. Ripple only limits the apps that are hard to put down, such as short video, short drama and games. Maps, calls, WeChat messages and reaching parents keep working. The first part below is for adults limiting their own phone, the second is for parents setting up a child's phone. Android is at v0.1.17 and iPhone at v0.1.5, and the features differ, so both are covered.

Adults: less short video on foot, eyes back on the road

Set daily limits and open limits on short video and short drama

Short video and short drama are the easiest to keep scrolling while walking. Set how long each can run per day, in custom minutes, or 0 minutes to block it all day. Add how many times it can open, and turn on the short wait before each open so you pause before pulling the phone out mid-walk. See the short drama page and the open limit page.

Block by schedule during your walk to and from work

Most walking time is at fixed hours, for example 8:00 to 8:30 am from home to the station and 6:00 to 6:30 pm back from the office. Add schedules for those times with only short video, short drama and games on the list. Leave maps, calls, WeChat messages and music off it, so asking the way, taking calls and replying to messages still work. For the ride itself, see the commute page.

Start one-tap focus before you head out

When the time is not fixed, tap one-tap focus on Android before you leave and pick about as long as the walk, say 15 minutes. Short video will not open until you get there. See the one-tap focus page.

WeChat Channels and Moments can start with a few minutes

On Android you can limit only Channels and Moments inside WeChat: start with a short allowance, then write a reason to extend, up to 3 times a day, counted toward the WeChat daily limit. In strict mode, extending needs the unlock code. WeChat chats and voice calls still work.

Turn on strict mode if you keep switching rules off

If you set rules and then turn them off halfway down the street, turn on strict mode. Give an unlock code of at least 8 digits to family, and loosening rules then needs their code. Strict mode also turns on Anti-Uninstall. If you forget the code, you can request release after 72 hours. See the strict mode page.

Parents: a child on the phone while walking to and from school

Set daily limits on games and short video, or 0 minutes

Kids on the road mostly play games and watch short video. Give those apps a daily limit so they stop opening when time is up, including on the way home. A game that is especially hard to stop can be set to 0 minutes to block it all day, then opened up on weekends. See the mobile games page.

Block the school route by schedule

Add two schedules on the child's phone, for example 7:00 to 8:00 am on the way to school and 4:30 to 5:30 pm on the way home, with games and short video on the list. For class time, see the school hours page.

Calls to parents and maps still work

On the way, kids may need to call or message a parent or check a map. Leave calls, WeChat and maps off the list so checking in on arrival still works. To limit only WeChat Channels, use the Android Channels allowance.

Turn on Anti-Uninstall; changing the clock does not help

Older kids learn to dig into settings. On Android turn on Anti-Uninstall, and strict mode if needed, with the code known only to parents. Ripple counts time since the phone started, so moving the clock back does not reset the limits.

If the child uses an iPhone

iPhone v0.1.5 can block apps by schedule, block deleting apps, protect against clock changes and use an unlock code, so school route schedules work. Daily limits, 0 minutes, open limits, one-tap focus, strict mode, Channels allowance and website blocking are Android only for now. See the parental control page.

Setup steps (Android v0.1.17)

Install or update to the latest version

Download the latest Android APK and turn on accessibility when asked. If you have an older build, update in Settings › About › Check for updates. On iPhone install v0.1.5, which needs iOS 16 or later.

Adult phone: daily limits, open limits and walking schedules on short video and short drama

Leave maps, calls, WeChat messages and music off the list. If the time is not fixed, use one-tap focus before you head out.

Child phone: daily limits or 0 minutes on games and short video, plus school route schedules

Leave calls, WeChat and maps off the list so the child can always reach a parent.

Set unlock codes and Anti-Uninstall on the child's phone

Give your own code to family, and keep the code on the child's phone with parents. Turn on Anti-Uninstall on Android, with strict mode if you want a firmer lock. On iPhone turn on the block on deleting apps.

FAQ

I keep looking down at short video while walking and miss my turn. What helps?

On Ripple for Android v0.1.17, set daily limits and open limits on short video and short drama, and add schedules for your walk to and from work so those apps will not open then. If the time is not fixed, start one-tap focus before you leave. Maps, calls and WeChat messages still work. For a firmer rule, turn on strict mode and give the unlock code to family.

My child plays games while walking to and from school and does not look at traffic. What can I set?

On the child's phone, give games and short video a daily limit or set a game to 0 minutes, and add schedules for the walk to and from school so games will not open then. Leave calls, WeChat and maps off the list so the child can still reach you. Keep the unlock code with parents and turn on Anti-Uninstall. Road safety habits still need regular reminders from parents.

Can I still use maps and make calls with limits on?

Yes. Ripple only limits the apps you put on the list. Leave maps, calls, WeChat messages and music off it and they keep working.

How is this different from the commute and school hours pages?

The commute page covers scrolling while seated on the subway or bus, and school hours covers class time. This page covers time on foot: adults walking to work or out for a walk, and kids walking to and from school. More answers are on the FAQ.

Does the iPhone version have these features?

iPhone v0.1.5 has schedules, the block on deleting apps, clock change protection and an unlock code. Daily limits, open limits, 0 minutes, one-tap focus, strict mode, Channels allowance and website blocking are Android only.

Does it cost money or need an account?

It is free with no ads and no account.
