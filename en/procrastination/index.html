Five minutes on the phone before you start, and an hour is gone

A lot of people do this. The laptop is open and the document is ready, but the hand picks up the phone first, just five minutes before starting. Five minutes turns into half an hour, then an hour, and the real work has not started. Kids do it too. The school bag goes down, they want to play a little before homework, and by the time they start it is late. The moment before you begin is the hardest, and the easier the phone is to pick up then, the longer you put things off. Ripple can lock distracting apps right when you are about to start, or make you wait a few seconds every time you open one. The first part is for adults setting this up for themselves and the second part is for parents. Android is on v0.1.17 and iPhone on v0.1.5, with different features, so they are described separately.

Adults: sit down and start without picking up the phone

Lock it the moment you start

Android has Quick Focus. Pick a length such as 45 minutes and tap once, and listed short video, short drama and social apps will not open for that time. You do not need to set a schedule in advance.

A fixed time to start every day

For writing, studying or exercise that happens every day, add a schedule such as 20:00 to 21:30. Listed apps close at that time, so you do not have to decide each day whether to begin.

The app you open just for a moment

Cap how many times your most opened app can open per day and turn on the pause before opening. During the pause the screen shows how many times you opened it today and how long you used it. Seeing that number is often enough to close it. See the open limit page.

Limit the total time too

Give short video apps a daily limit in minutes, for example 30 minutes a day, and they stay closed once it is used up. The one you can never put down can be set to 0 minutes so it does not open all day.

If you tend to undo your rules

Turn on strict mode on Android. Rules can then only be loosened with the unlock code. In strict mode the code is at least 8 characters and works at most 3 times a day. Give it to a family member or friend. Strict mode turns on Anti-Uninstall too, and if you forget the code you can request removal, which takes effect after 72 hours.

If you use an iPhone

iPhone v0.1.5 blocks apps by schedule, so you can add a schedule for the time you start work each day and short videos will not open then. It can also block deleting apps, protect against clock changes and use an unlock code. It has no Quick Focus, daily limits, open limits or strict mode.

Parents: the child always wants to play a bit before homework

Between getting home and homework

Kids who play first often cannot stop. Agree on a start time with your child, such as 18:30, and add a homework schedule so games and short videos close then. See the homework page.

Start early when there is a lot to do

On a heavy homework day, a parent can tap Quick Focus on Android when the child sits down, for example 60 minutes, and listed apps stay closed for that time without waiting for the schedule.

Agree how long playing first lasts

If the deal is 20 minutes of play before homework, give games and short videos a daily limit in minutes. Once it is used up the apps stay closed for the day, so there is less bargaining. A game you do not want them to play can be set to 0 minutes.

The child cannot change it

On Android with strict mode, rules can only be loosened with the unlock code, which the parent keeps. With Anti-Uninstall on, the child cannot remove Ripple. Ripple on Android counts time since boot, so changing the system clock does not add time. Listed apps are also blocked in split screen, floating windows and PiP. On OPPO phones a floating window can only be covered and muted, and it has to be closed by hand.

Keep alive settings on Chinese brand phones

Xiaomi, OPPO, vivo, Huawei and Honor phones may stop Ripple in the background. After installing, follow the keep alive guide to set autostart and battery optimization.

If the child uses an iPhone or iPad

iPhone v0.1.5 blocks apps by schedule, so games and short videos do not open during homework time. It can also block deleting apps, protect against clock changes and use an unlock code. It has no Quick Focus or daily limits. See the parental control page.

Setup steps

Install Ripple

On Android download the latest APK and turn on the accessibility service as guided. On iPhone install v0.1.5, which needs iOS 16 or later.

Pick the apps that hold you up

Put short videos, short dramas, games and social feeds on the list. Leave work and study apps off.

Lock them when you start

On Android use Quick Focus for a one off start and schedules for daily tasks. On iPhone use schedules.

Set open limits and daily limits, hand over the code

On Android give your most opened app an open limit with a pause before opening, then a daily limit. Give the unlock code to a family member or friend, and keep the code for a child's phone yourself.

FAQ

I always scroll before starting real work and cannot stop. What can I do?

When you are about to start, tap Quick Focus in Ripple on Android and pick a length, and listed short video apps will not open for that time. Give your most opened app an open limit and a pause before opening, and give short videos a daily limit.

My child always wants to play before homework. What can I do?

Agree on a start time, add a homework schedule, and games and short videos close at that time. If you agreed on some play time first, give games a daily limit. On Android turn on strict mode and Anti-Uninstall and keep the unlock code.

What is the difference between Quick Focus and a schedule?

A schedule is set in advance and starts at the same time every day. Quick Focus starts right away for a length you pick, which helps on days you want to start early. Quick Focus is Android only for now.

Does it work on iPhone?

iPhone v0.1.5 blocks selected apps by schedule, can block deleting apps, protects against clock changes and has an unlock code. It has no Quick Focus, daily limits or open limits.

Will apps I need for work or study be locked?

Not if they are off the list. Only Complete Lock in a schedule leaves just the system essential apps, and then work and study apps will not open either.

Is it free? Do I need an account?

It is free with no ads and no account. See the FAQ for more.
