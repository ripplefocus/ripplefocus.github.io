Less phone scrolling while doing chores

After dinner you wash the dishes with the phone leaning by the sink, and when one short video ends your hand swipes to the next. Halfway through folding laundry you sit down to finish an episode of a short drama, and the clothes stay on the bed. On Saturday you plan to clean in the morning, pick up the phone for two minutes, and look up close to noon. Kids do much the same. Ask them to tidy their room, pack the school bag or wash their own socks, and the tablet goes up on the desk while they work, or they say they will clean after this round. The site has a cooking page for time in the kitchen and a weekends page for lying around at home. This page covers dishes, mopping, laundry and tidying up. Ripple only manages apps on phones and tablets. Whether the chores get done is up to the family, and what Ripple can do is keep short video and games closed during that time. The first part below is for adults limiting their own phone, the second is for parents setting up a child's tablet or phone. Android is at v0.1.17 and iPhone at v0.1.5, and the features differ, so both are covered.

Adults: fewer videos while the work gets done

Start one-tap focus before you begin

Dishes and mopping usually take half an hour to an hour. Before you start, tap one-tap focus on Android and pick a length that fits the job, for example 1 hour. Choose Lock only the apps I pick and select short video, short drama and games. Leave music, audiobooks and WeChat unpicked, so you can still listen to music while you work. See the one-tap focus page.

Set daily limits and open limits on short video and short drama

Chores come every day, and starting focus each time is easy to forget. Give short video and short drama a daily limit in custom minutes, or 0 minutes to block them all day. Add how many times they can open and turn on the short wait before each open, so when you reach for the phone halfway through mopping, you pause first. See the short drama page and the open limit page.

Use a schedule for a fixed cleaning time

Many families clean on Saturday morning. Add a Saturday morning schedule with only short video, short drama and games on the list. Those apps will not open during it and come back when it ends.

WeChat Channels and Moments can start with a few minutes

On Android you can limit only Channels and Moments inside WeChat: start with a short allowance, then write a reason to extend, up to 3 times a day, counted toward the WeChat daily limit. In strict mode, extending needs the unlock code. WeChat chats and voice calls keep working.

Turn on strict mode if you tend to switch it off

If you switch the rules off when you get tired of the work, turn on strict mode. Give an unlock code of at least 8 digits to someone at home, so loosening the rules means asking them. The unlock code can succeed at most 3 times a day. Strict mode turns on Anti-Uninstall too. If the code is lost, you can request removal after 72 hours. See the strict mode page.

Parents: a tablet playing while the child tidies up

Set daily limits on the child's tablet or old phone

On the Android tablet or old phone your child uses, give games and short video a daily limit. When it runs out they will not open. If one game is hard to put down, set it to 0 minutes for now and change it back once the room is tidy. See the mobile games page.

Add a schedule for weekend chores

If the family cleans together on Saturday morning, add the same schedule on the child's device with games and short video on the list. They will not open during it and come back when it ends. Keep study apps and the apps used to reach parents off the list.

Split screen, floating windows and picture-in-picture are blocked too

Some kids drag a video into a floating window and watch while folding clothes. On Android, listed apps are blocked in split screen, floating windows and picture-in-picture, and that time counts toward the day. On OPPO phones a floating window can only be covered and muted, so the window itself has to be closed by hand. See the split screen page.

Parents keep the unlock code, with Anti-Uninstall and clock change protection

Only parents know the unlock code. Turn on Anti-Uninstall on Android so the child cannot delete Ripple. Ripple for Android counts time since the phone started, so moving the system clock forward does not add time. See the clock change page.

If the child uses an iPhone

iPhone v0.1.5 has schedules, the block on deleting apps, clock change protection and an unlock code, so the weekend cleaning schedule works there too. Daily limits, 0 minutes, open limits, one-tap focus, strict mode and Channels allowance are Android only for now. See the parental control overview.

Setup steps (Android v0.1.17)

Install or update to the latest version

Download the latest Android APK and turn on the Accessibility Service as guided. If you have an older version, update in Settings › About › Check for updates. On iPhone install v0.1.5, which needs iOS 16 or later.

Adult phone: start one-tap focus before chores, or set daily limits and open limits on short video and short drama

Keep music, audiobooks and WeChat off the list. Add a schedule if cleaning happens at a fixed time.

Child's tablet or old phone: daily limits or 0 minutes on games and short video, plus a chores schedule

Keep study apps and the apps used to reach parents off the list.

Set an unlock code and turn on Anti-Uninstall on the child's device

Adults give their unlock code to family. Parents keep the code for the child's device. Turn on Anti-Uninstall on Android, or strict mode for a firmer lock. On iPhone turn on the block on deleting apps.

FAQ

I keep watching short videos while washing dishes or mopping and stop halfway. What helps?

On Ripple for Android v0.1.17, start one-tap focus before you begin and lock only short video, short drama and games for the length of the job. Since chores come every day, you can also give those apps daily limits and open limits. Music and WeChat still work. For a firmer rule, turn on strict mode and give the unlock code to family.

My child watches videos on a tablet while tidying the room. What can I set?

On the child's Android tablet or old phone, give games and short video a daily limit or set them to 0 minutes for now. Add a schedule for fixed weekend cleaning. Split screen, floating windows and picture-in-picture are blocked too. Parents keep the unlock code and turn on Anti-Uninstall, and changing the clock does not help.

Can Ripple make my child do chores?

No. Ripple only manages apps on phones and tablets. It can keep short video and games closed during that time. How chores are shared and whether they get done is for the family to agree on.

How is this different from the cooking and weekends pages?

The cooking page covers time in the kitchen and weekends covers lying around at home all day. This page covers dishes, mopping, laundry and tidying up. More answers are on the FAQ.

Does the iPhone version have these features?

iPhone v0.1.5 has schedules, the block on deleting apps, clock change protection and an unlock code. Daily limits, open limits, 0 minutes, one-tap focus, strict mode, Channels allowance and website blocking are Android only.

Does it cost money or need an account?

It is free with no ads and no account.
