Ripple vs Zenze and Mobicip

Zenze shows up when adults search for a way to limit their own apps on Android. Mobicip shows up when parents search for a parental control that works on both iPhone and Android. Ripple is a free app with no account, used by adults on their own phone and by parents on a child's phone. I make Ripple, so read this with that in mind. Ripple for Android is v0.1.29 and Ripple for iPhone is v0.1.5. Facts about Zenze come from its Google Play listing, and facts about Mobicip come from mobicip.com, both read in November 2026.

What each one is

Zenze

An Android app from Atmana Tech. It sets time limits for single apps and websites, schedules limits for work hours or sleep, and gives you a daily Productivity Score. It also has a community with leaderboards and rewards for staying under your limits. Blocking adult websites is optional and uses a VPN. The Play listing says it is free, contains ads and has in app purchases from $2.99 to $49.99. I did not find an iPhone version.

Mobicip

A paid parental control service for iPhone, iPad, Android, Kindle Fire, Windows, Mac and Chromebook. The parent manages everything from their own account: app blocking, website filtering, screen time limits, schedules, remote lock, location and geofences, activity reports and uninstall protection. App time limits and social media alerts are listed on its plans. Plans are billed yearly at $2.99, $4.99 or $7.99 a month for 5, 10 or 20 devices, with a 7 day free trial.

Ripple

A free app with no ads, no account and no subscription. On Android (8.0 or later) it has schedules, a daily amount per app including 0 minutes, open limits, a break rule, quick focus, blocked websites, a usage calendar, a mode that limits only WeChat Channels and Moments, and strict mode. On iPhone v0.1.5 it only blocks by schedule, stops apps being deleted, protects the clock and uses an unlock code.

For your own phone

A daily cap on a few apps

Zenze and Ripple both do this on Android. Zenze adds a score and a leaderboard if competing with other people keeps you going. Ripple has no score, no ads and nothing to sign up for. Each app simply gets its own amount, for example 30 minutes for a video app and 0 minutes for a game on workdays.

Making it hard to back out

In Ripple, loosening a rule or removing the app needs a 15 minute wait or the unlock code. With strict mode only the code works, at most 3 successful uses a day, so you can hand the code to a partner or friend. Check the current Zenze listing for how easy its limits are to turn off.

Opening an app too often

Ripple for Android can cap how many times a day an app opens and can ask for a short break after a stretch of continuous use. I did not see open limits on the Zenze listing.

Using an iPhone

Mobicip runs on iPhone but is made for parents managing a child. Ripple for iPhone v0.1.5 only blocks apps by schedule with an unlock code. Screen Time in iOS settings is free and already has daily app limits.

For a child's phone

Managing from your own phone

Mobicip does this. You see reports, location and new installs from your account and can lock the child's device remotely. Ripple has no parent app, no reports and no location, and rules are set on the child's phone in person. Zenze is aimed at adults.

Blocking one game on a child's Android phone

Set the game to 0 minutes in Ripple and it will not open all day while other apps keep their amounts. Turn on strict mode and keep the unlock code. Anti uninstall turns on with strict mode and needs Android 11 or later. A forgotten code is lifted 72 hours after a request.

Homework and bedtime

Ripple schedules run on their own, for example no games or short video from 7 pm to 9 pm on school days and nothing but calls after 10 pm. Mobicip has managed schedules too and adds web filtering on every browser.

Child on iPhone

Mobicip covers iPhone with filtering and limits. Ripple for iPhone v0.1.5 only blocks apps by schedule with a code the parent keeps, and it has no daily amounts. Screen Time with Family Sharing is free and does more for most families.

How to choose

Choose Zenze

You are an adult on Android, you like a score and a leaderboard to keep you going, and ads do not bother you.

Choose Mobicip

You want to manage several kids and devices from your own phone, with web filtering, location and reports, and you are fine paying yearly.

Choose Ripple

You want plain daily caps and schedules for each app with no account, no ads and no payment, and an unlock code someone else can keep, for yourself or a child's Android phone.

Try it first

Install the latest APK, turn on the accessibility service when asked, give one app 30 minutes a day and open it to see the block screen.

FAQ

Is Ripple a full parental control like Mobicip?

No. It has no remote control, no web content filter by category, no reports and no location. It only limits apps and websites on the phone it is installed on.

Does Ripple need an account?

No. Rules are set and kept on the phone.

Is Ripple really free?

Yes. No ads, no subscription and no account. More in the FAQ.

What does the iPhone version do?

Ripple for iPhone v0.1.5 blocks apps by schedule, blocks deleting apps, protects the clock and uses an unlock code. It has no daily amounts.
