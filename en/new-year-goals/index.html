A New Year goal to use your phone less, and a phone agreement with your kids

A lot of people write down "use my phone less" at the end of the year and are back to old habits a week or two later. Parents often agree on phone rules with their kids around New Year or before the winter break, and soon nobody remembers them. A goal that only lives in your head is hard to keep. Putting it into the phone helps. Ripple turns "scroll less" into numbers and times: how long per day, how many opens per day, and after what hour an app stays closed. The first part is for adults setting their own goal and the second part is for parents making an agreement with their kids. Android is on v0.1.18 and iPhone on v0.1.5, with different features, so they are described separately.

Adults: turn the goal into rules

Check how much you use now

Your phone's built in screen time shows how long you spend in each app. Pick the two or three you use most and aim the goal at those. That is easier to keep than quitting everything at once.

Make "less" a number

On Android give those apps a daily limit in minutes, for example 45 minutes of short video a day, after which they stay closed for the day. Start a bit loose in the first month and lower it the next.

Stop the automatic opens

For an app you open dozens of times a day, set a daily open limit and the pause before opening, which first shows how many times you opened it today and how long you used it. See the open limit page.

Keep whole blocks for sleep and work

Add schedules such as 23:00 to 7:00 every day and weekday mornings, so apps on the list close at those times. Alarm and calls still work.

Worried you will undo it in a week

Turn on strict mode, so the unlock code is the only way to loosen rules. In strict mode the code is at least 8 characters with at most 3 successful uses a day, and a family member or friend can keep it. Strict mode also turns on Anti-Uninstall. If you forget the code you can request removal, which takes effect after 72 hours.

On iPhone

iPhone v0.1.5 blocks apps by schedule, for example short video and games after 11 p.m. It can also block deleting apps, protect against clock changes and use an unlock code. It has no daily limits, open limits, pause before opening, Quick Focus, Complete Lock or strict mode.

Parents: a phone agreement for the new year

Let your child name a goal first

Kids push back when rules just arrive from above. Around New Year or before the winter break, sit down together and let your child say how they want to use the phone, such as how long to play at weekends and when to go to bed. Add your part, agree, and put it on paper on the wall.

Put the agreement on their phone

On your child's Android phone or tablet give games and short video apps a daily limit, for example 30 minutes on school days and 90 minutes at weekends. Schedules can apply to school days only or weekends only, and homework and sleep hours become schedules. See the homework page.

Pause one game for a while

If the agreement says a game is off for this term, set it to 0 minutes so it stays closed all day, and change it back on the agreed date.

Stricter during exam weeks

Turn on Complete Lock for revision hours. Only essential system apps such as phone, messages and alarm work, and the unlock code cannot end it. From v0.1.18 a request to loosen rules sent before the lock started also cannot be confirmed while the lock is on.

Your child cannot change it

Turn on strict mode and keep the unlock code yourself. With Anti-Uninstall on, your child cannot delete Ripple. Android counts time since boot, so changing the system clock gives no extra time. On Xiaomi, OPPO, vivo, Huawei and Honor phones follow the keep alive guide for auto start and battery settings.

Child on iPhone or iPad

iPhone v0.1.5 blocks apps by schedule, so the apps stay closed during homework and sleep hours. It can block deleting apps, protect against clock changes and use an unlock code, with no daily limits or open limits. See the parental control page.

Setup steps

Install Ripple

On Android download the latest APK and turn on the accessibility service as guided. On iPhone install v0.1.5, which needs iOS 16 or later.

Pick the apps

Only the few you use most and find hardest to put down. Keep WeChat, phone, maps, payment and study apps off the list.

Write the goal as numbers

On Android set daily limits and open limits, then add schedules that match your day. On iPhone block by schedule.

Review once a month

At the start of each month check how it went and lower the limit a little. In strict mode changing rules needs the unlock code.

FAQ

My New Year goal is to use my phone less. How do I stick to it?

Turn the goal into rules. In Ripple give your most used apps a daily limit and a daily open limit, and add schedules for sleep and work. On Android turn on strict mode and give the unlock code to a family member or friend.

What if a strict goal is too hard from day one?

Start looser in the first month, for example a third less than now, then lower it each month. Android daily limits are set in minutes, from 1 minute to 12 hours.

How do I make a new year phone agreement with my child?

Let your child share their view first, then agree on how long to play on school days and at weekends and when to sleep. Then set daily limits and schedules on their Android phone, turn on strict mode and Anti-Uninstall, and keep the unlock code yourself.

Can it be stricter during exam weeks?

Yes. On Android turn on Complete Lock for revision hours. Only essential system apps such as phone, messages and alarm work, and the unlock code cannot end it.

Does this work on iPhone?

iPhone v0.1.5 blocks selected apps by schedule, can block deleting apps, protects against clock changes and has an unlock code. It has no daily limits, open limits, Complete Lock or strict mode.

Is it free? Do I need an account?

It is free with no ads and no account. More in the FAQ.
