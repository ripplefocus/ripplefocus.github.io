Everyone on their phone when you meet friends, and kids gaming all afternoon with classmates

You finally get friends together for dinner, and before the food arrives everyone at the table is looking at a phone. Kids are much the same. Classmates come over, and they sit on the sofa gaming on a phone the whole afternoon without doing homework or going outside. This page first covers adults meeting friends, then what parents can set when kids hang out with classmates. Android is on v0.1.18 and iPhone on v0.1.5, with different features, so they are described separately.

Adults: less phone when you meet friends

Lock a few apps when you sit down

Once you sit down at the restaurant, Android Quick Focus locks the phone right away, Pick 90 minutes and it ends on its own around when dinner is over. Choose to lock only the apps you pick, such as short video and games, so WeChat keeps working. Calls always work, so you will not miss anything urgent from home.

Make regular meetups a schedule

For regular plans such as basketball with friends on Friday evenings or a monthly class dinner, add a schedule with short video, games and live streaming on the list, so they stay closed during those hours. Leave WeChat or your chat app off the list so you can still agree on a place and share your location.

No catching up on the way home

On the way home it is easy to scroll through everything you missed. On Android give short video a daily limit, for example 40 minutes a day, after which it stays closed for the day. You can also set a daily open limit and the pause before opening. See the open limit page.

If you might undo it

With strict mode on, the unlock code is the only way to loosen rules. In strict mode the code is at least 8 characters with at most 3 successful uses a day, and a friend or someone at home can keep it. Strict mode also turns on Anti-Uninstall. If you forget the code you can request removal, which takes effect after 72 hours.

Adults on iPhone

iPhone v0.1.5 blocks apps by schedule, for example short video and games during your Friday evening meetup. It can also block deleting apps, protect against clock changes and use an unlock code. It has no daily limits, open limits, pause before opening, Quick Focus, Complete Lock or strict mode.

Kids: classmates over or out together

Agree on how long first

Before classmates come over, agree with your child how long they can game today and what to do with the rest of the time, such as playing ball outside or building something. On your child's Android phone or tablet give games a daily limit, for example 90 minutes at weekends. When it runs out the games stay closed for the day, so you do not have to keep reminding them.

A schedule for the afternoon they visit

If classmates usually come on weekend afternoons, add a schedule such as Saturday 14:00 to 17:00 with games and short video on the list. If you are fine with some gaming, leave the schedule off and let the daily limit stop it. More on the kids and games page.

When they go out together

When your child goes to the park or the mall with classmates, the phone is handy for staying in touch. Games and short video still follow the daily limit and schedules, while calls and chat apps left off the list keep working, so you can reach them.

Your child cannot change it

Turn on strict mode on their phone and keep the unlock code yourself. With Anti-Uninstall on, your child cannot delete Ripple. Android counts time since boot, so changing the system clock gives no extra time. On Xiaomi, OPPO, vivo, Huawei and Honor phones follow the keep alive guide for auto start and battery settings.

Child on iPhone or iPad

iPhone v0.1.5 blocks apps by schedule, so games stay closed in the hours classmates are over. It can block deleting apps, protect against clock changes and use an unlock code, with no daily limits or open limits. See the parental control page.

Setup steps

Install Ripple

On Android download the latest APK and turn on the accessibility service as guided. On iPhone install v0.1.5, which needs iOS 16 or later.

Set up your own phone

Use Quick Focus before a meetup, add a schedule for regular meetups, and give short video a daily limit.

Set up your child's phone

Give games the daily limit you agreed on, add a schedule for the afternoons classmates visit, and turn on strict mode and Anti-Uninstall.

Adjust after a few times

After a few meetups see whether it fits and loosen or tighten it. In strict mode changing rules needs the unlock code.

FAQ

I keep checking my phone at dinner with friends. What can I do?

On Android start Quick Focus when you sit down and lock only short video and games, so calls and WeChat keep working. For regular meetups add a schedule with short video and games on the list. On iPhone v0.1.5 use a schedule.

If my phone is locked, can my friends still reach me?

Leave WeChat or your chat app off the list and it keeps working. Calls are not affected either.

Classmates come over and they game all afternoon. What can I do?

On your child's Android phone or tablet give games a daily limit, for example 90 minutes at weekends, after which they stay closed for the day. You can also add a schedule for the afternoons classmates usually visit.

Can my child just keep playing on a classmate's phone?

Ripple only manages the phone it is installed on. It cannot control a classmate's phone, so agree on how long they play with your child and the other parents beforehand.

Can my child turn it off?

With strict mode on, loosening rules needs the unlock code and Anti-Uninstall is on, so your child cannot delete Ripple.

Does this work on iPhone?

iPhone v0.1.5 blocks selected apps by schedule, can block deleting apps, protects against clock changes and has an unlock code. It has no daily limits, open limits, Quick Focus, Complete Lock or strict mode.

Is it free? Do I need an account?

It is free with no ads and no account. More in the FAQ.
