Picking up the phone while writing a thesis or report, and kids who drift to videos after two lines of an essay

Writing is when the phone is hardest to leave alone. A sentence will not come, you open a short video, and half an hour later the train of thought is gone. Students on a thesis, office workers with a weekly report or proposal, and freelancers writing copy all run into this. Kids do it too. They cannot start an essay, reach for the phone to look something up, and end up watching videos. This page first covers adults setting limits on their own phone while they write, then parents setting limits on a child's phone during essay and journal time. Android is on v0.1.18 and iPhone on v0.1.5, with different features, so they are covered separately.

Adults: theses, reports, proposals and copy

A fixed writing block each day

If you write your thesis from 9 to 11 every morning, add a schedule for those hours with short video, short dramas, social apps and games on the list. They stay closed until it ends. Leave the browser, reference and dictionary apps and your messaging app off the list.

Lock when you sit down

For an unplanned writing session, Quick Focus locks the phone at once. Choose to lock only the apps you pick and set 45 minutes or an hour and a half. Calls always work.

Breaks are fine, with an amount

A short break between paragraphs is normal. Give short video a daily limit, for example 30 minutes, after which it stays closed for the day. For an app you open without thinking, set a daily open limit and the pause before opening. See the open limit page.

Shut it off before a deadline

In the days before a deadline or a thesis defense, set the most distracting app to 0 minutes so it does not open at all that day, and change it back after you submit.

If you might change the settings

With strict mode on, the unlock code is the only way to loosen rules. In strict mode the code is at least 8 characters with at most 3 successful uses a day, and a roommate or family member can keep it. Strict mode also turns on Anti-Uninstall. If you forget the code you can request removal, which takes effect after 72 hours.

Adults on iPhone

iPhone v0.1.5 blocks apps by schedule, for example short video during a two hour morning writing block. It can also block deleting apps, protect against clock changes and use an unlock code. It has no daily limits, open limits, pause before opening, Quick Focus, Complete Lock or strict mode.

Kids: essays, journals and book reports

Cover it with homework time

Most essay and journal writing happens during homework, so add a schedule from 18:30 to 20:30 with games, short video and cartoon apps on the list. More on the homework page.

Reference apps still work

If your child needs a dictionary or sample essays, keep dictionary and study apps off the list and block only games and short video. See the study apps page.

Longer weekend assignments

For a weekend book report, add a separate weekend morning schedule. Give games and short video a daily limit, for example 40 minutes a day, so play comes after writing and stops when the time is used.

Kids cannot undo it

Turn on strict mode and keep the unlock code to yourself. With Anti-Uninstall on, your child cannot delete Ripple. Android counts time since boot, so changing the system clock gives no extra time. On Xiaomi, OPPO, vivo, Huawei and Honor phones follow the keep alive guide for auto start and battery settings.

Child on iPhone or iPad

iPhone v0.1.5 blocks apps by schedule, so games and short video stay closed during homework time. It can block deleting apps, protect against clock changes and use an unlock code, with no daily limits or open limits. Ripple has no remote management, so set it up on the child's device. See the parental control page.

Setup steps

Install Ripple

On Android download the latest APK and turn on the accessibility service as guided. On iPhone install v0.1.5, which needs iOS 16 or later.

Set up your own phone

Add a schedule for your daily writing time, give short video a daily limit, use Quick Focus for unplanned sessions, and set the most distracting app to 0 minutes before a deadline.

Set up your child's phone

Add a homework schedule, keep dictionary and study apps off the list, set daily limits for games and short video, then turn on strict mode and Anti-Uninstall and keep the code yourself.

Check after a week

After a week look at how much got written and adjust the schedules and limits.

FAQ

I keep picking up the phone while writing my thesis. What can I do?

On Android add a schedule for your writing hours with short video, social apps and games on the list, use Quick Focus to lock only chosen apps when you sit down, and give short video a daily limit. On iPhone v0.1.5 use schedules.

I need to look things up while writing. Will those apps be blocked?

No. Only apps on the list are blocked, so the browser, reference and dictionary apps and messaging keep working if you leave them off.

Can I block one app completely before a deadline?

On Android set it to 0 minutes and it will not open that day. With strict mode on, changing it back needs the unlock code.

My child writes two lines of an essay and goes to the phone. What can I do?

On your child's Android phone or tablet add a homework schedule that blocks only games and short video, keep dictionary and study apps available, set a daily limit for games, and turn on strict mode and Anti-Uninstall.

Can I set it up remotely from my own phone?

Not at the moment. Ripple's settings stay on that phone and there is no remote management, so set it up on your child's phone directly.

Does this work on iPhone?

iPhone v0.1.5 blocks selected apps by schedule, can block deleting apps, protects against clock changes and has an unlock code. It has no daily limits, open limits, Quick Focus, Complete Lock or strict mode.

Is it free? Do I need an account?

It is free with no ads and no account. More in the FAQ.
