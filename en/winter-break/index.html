Winter break at home, and the phone is on from morning to night

Winter break is short on daylight and long on indoor hours. A kid does an hour of holiday homework, then the tablet comes out and stays out. Adults on year-end leave often end up the same way on the sofa. I make Ripple, a free app blocker with no account. During school weeks your rules are built around the school day. Over the break they need a different shape, and this page shows how I would set that up on Android v0.1.30 and iPhone v0.1.5.

What changes over the break

No school day to lean on

School hours used to keep the phone away for most of the day. Over the break the whole day is free, so a daily time limit matters more than a schedule.

Mornings drift

Kids who sleep in often start the day on the phone in bed. A morning schedule that keeps games and short videos closed until homework time helps.

Holidays in the middle

New Year and family visits sit in the middle of the break. On Android you can give different days different rules, so a holiday can be looser than a homework day.

On your own phone

A daily budget for the leave

Give short video and game apps a daily time limit, and a daily open limit if you tend to open them many times for a minute each.

Quick Focus for a few hours

When you want to read, tidy up or go out without the phone pulling at you, Quick Focus on Android locks only the apps you pick for a set time. Maps, the camera and calls keep working.

Keep WeChat, drop Channels

On Android you can close only WeChat Channels and Moments and keep chats and payments, which matters during holiday visits.

Loosening takes a wait

Loosening a rule needs a wait (15 minutes by default, or 30 minutes, 1 hour or 2 hours) or the unlock code. Strict mode leaves only the code.

On a child's phone

Homework first in the morning

Set a weekday schedule on the child's phone that keeps games and short videos closed from waking up until the homework block is done, then a daily time limit for the afternoon.

Looser on holiday days

On Android, give New Year days or visiting days their own times by choosing which weekdays a rule applies to, or turn a schedule off for those days and back on after.

Uninstall and clock protection

Ripple can block uninstalling itself and stop changes to the phone clock, so a kid cannot get extra time by moving the date forward. iPhone v0.1.5 has both as well.

Keep the code

Set the rules and the unlock code on the child's phone and keep the code yourself.

What Ripple cannot do

There is no remote management. You set everything on the child's phone and cannot see or change it from your own phone.

Setting it up

Install

Android: install the latest APK. iPhone: install v0.1.5 (iOS 16 or later) and allow Screen Time access.

Pick the apps

Choose the games, short video and social apps that take most of the day.

Add a morning schedule

Pick the days and the hours. On iPhone v0.1.5 this is the main tool: apps are blocked by Screen Time during the hours you set.

Add a daily limit on Android

Set minutes per day, and an open limit if needed, for the afternoon and evening.

Set the unlock code

Keep it away from the person using the phone. Turn on strict mode if the wait is too easy to sit out.

FAQ

Can iPhone do the daily limit too?

No. Ripple for iPhone v0.1.5 blocks apps on a schedule through Screen Time, with uninstall protection, clock change protection and an unlock code. Daily time limits, open limits, Quick Focus and the WeChat Channels block are Android only.

Can I see my kid's usage from my phone?

No. There is no remote management, and rules are kept on the child's phone.

Will a kid get around it by changing the date?

Ripple watches for clock changes on both Android and iPhone. On some Android phones a blocked app may still flash on screen for a moment before the block screen covers it.

Does it cost anything?

No. Ripple is free, has no ads and no in-app payments.
