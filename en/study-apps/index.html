You open your phone to study and end up somewhere else

Vocabulary, language practice, lessons and research all happen on the phone now. The trouble is that the study app and the short video app live on the same phone. You open it to review twenty words, see a notification, tap it, and half an hour is gone. Kids are the same. They look up homework or do exercises in a study app, and a minute later they are in videos or games. Taking the phone away does not work, because the studying needs the phone. Ripple can limit only the few apps that distract you. Study apps stay off the list and keep working. The first part is for adults studying on their own and the second part is for parents whose kids study on a phone. Android is on v0.1.20 and iPhone on v0.1.5, with different features, so they are described separately.

Adults: study on the phone without drifting off

Leave study apps alone, limit the distracting ones

Ripple only limits the apps you put on the list. Vocabulary, language, course and reading apps stay off the list and open any time. Short videos, short dramas and social feeds go on the list and do not open during study time.

Keep a fixed study time

For example 7:00 to 7:40 in the morning and 20:00 to 21:30 in the evening. Add those as schedules and listed apps will not open then. Schedules can be set by weekday, with a different one for weekends.

Keep the rest of the day in check

Give short video apps a daily limit in minutes, for example 30 minutes a day, and they stay closed once it is used up. The one you can never put down can be set to 0 minutes so it does not open all day.

The quick check habit

If you keep switching out to take a look, cap how many times that app can open per day and turn on the pause before opening. During the pause the screen shows how many times you opened it today and how long you used it, and often you just close it. See the open limit page.

Exam prep that needs to hold

If you tend to undo your own rules, turn on strict mode on Android. Rules can then only be loosened with the unlock code. In strict mode the code is at least 8 characters and works at most 3 times a day. Give it to a family member or friend. Strict mode turns on Anti-Uninstall too, and if you forget the code you can request removal, which takes effect after 72 hours.

If you use an iPhone

iPhone v0.1.5 blocks apps by schedule, so short videos do not open during study time while unselected study apps keep working. It can also block deleting apps, protect against clock changes and use an unlock code. It has no daily limits, open limits or strict mode.

Parents: the child needs the phone to study but keeps switching to play

Keep study apps, list games and videos

Study apps, dictionaries and the teacher's video lessons stay off the list. Games, short videos and short dramas go on it and do not open during the homework schedule. See the homework page for that schedule.

During online classes

The class app works as usual, while games and short videos are closed for the same hours, so switching out does not help. See the online class page for details.

How long to play after studying

Agree with your child how long they can play each day and give games and short videos a daily limit, such as 30 minutes on school days and 1 hour on weekends. A game you do not want them to play can be set to 0 minutes.

The child cannot change it

On Android with strict mode on, rules can only be loosened with the unlock code, which the parent keeps. With Anti-Uninstall on the child cannot delete Ripple. Ripple on Android counts time since boot, so changing the system clock gives no extra time. Listed apps are also blocked in split screen, floating windows and picture in picture. On OPPO phones a floating window can only be covered and muted and has to be closed by hand.

Keep alive settings on Chinese brand phones

Xiaomi, OPPO, vivo, Huawei and Honor phones may stop Ripple in the background. After installing, follow the keep alive guide to allow autostart and turn off battery optimization.

If the child uses an iPhone or iPad

iPhone v0.1.5 blocks apps by schedule. Select only games and short videos for the homework schedule and leave study apps unselected. It can also block deleting apps, protect against clock changes and use an unlock code. It has no daily limits. See the parental control page for an overview.

How to set it up

Install Ripple

On Android download the latest APK and turn on the accessibility service as the app guides you. On iPhone install v0.1.5, which needs iOS 16 or later.

Decide which apps to limit

Study apps stay off the list. Short videos, short dramas, games and social feeds go on it.

Add a study schedule and daily limits

Add a study or homework schedule so listed apps do not open then. On Android also give them a daily limit or 0 minutes. On iPhone use schedules.

Set an unlock code

Adults give the code to a family member or friend, and parents keep the code for a child's phone. On Android turn on strict mode for a firmer setup, and on iPhone turn on block deleting apps.

FAQ

I open my phone to review vocabulary and end up in short videos. What can I do?

Put only the distracting apps such as short videos and short dramas on Ripple's list and leave study apps off it. Add a study schedule so listed apps do not open then. On Android you can also give short video apps a daily limit and an open limit.

My child uses the phone for homework and study apps. How do I stop them switching to games?

Leave study apps off the list, put games and short videos on it, and add a homework schedule. On Android turn on strict mode and Anti-Uninstall and keep the unlock code yourself, so the child cannot change or delete it.

Will study apps be blocked?

No. Ripple only blocks the apps on your list. If a schedule uses Complete Lock, only system essential apps work and study apps will not open either, so do not use Complete Lock when you need the phone to study.

Does this work on iPhone?

iPhone v0.1.5 blocks selected apps by schedule, so unselected study apps keep working. It can also block deleting apps, protect against clock changes and use an unlock code. It has no daily limits or open limits.

Can video websites in a browser be blocked?

Android has had website limits since v0.1.12. Add the video site and Ripple covers it with a block screen when it is opened in a browser. This was tested on Chrome in an emulator. Some Chinese brand built in browsers, UC and QQ Browser have not been checked on real phones and may not be blocked. See the website limits page. The iPhone app does not have this.

Is it free? Do I need an account?

It is free with no ads and no account. See the FAQ for more.
