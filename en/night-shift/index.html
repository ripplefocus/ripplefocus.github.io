Scrolling instead of sleeping after a night shift, and kids up on the phone while you work nights

After a night shift you get home in the morning and should go straight to sleep. You lie down, pick up the phone to relax for a minute, and it is noon. You wake up groggy in the afternoon and go back to work in the evening. Nurses, factory line workers, security guards, call center staff and ride hailing drivers know this well. With kids it gets harder. A parent leaves for the night shift, the child is home alone or with grandparents, nobody is watching, and the phone stays on until one or two in the morning before a school day. This page first covers adults setting limits around their shifts, then what parents can set before they leave for work. Android is on v0.1.18 and iPhone on v0.1.5, with different features, so they are described separately.

Adults: night or rotating shifts

A sleep schedule that starts when you get home

If your shift ends at 8 a.m., add a schedule from 8:30 to 14:30 with short video, short dramas, live streams and games on the list. They stay closed during those hours, so lying down means sleeping. Leave the alarm, phone and messaging apps off the list so work can still reach you.

Several schedules for rotating shifts

With rotating shifts, add one schedule for daytime sleep after nights and one for nighttime sleep after day shifts. Turn on the one that matches this week and pause the other. More on the bedtime page.

Set an amount for scrolling

Some time to unwind after work is fine. Give short video a daily limit, for example 30 minutes, after which it stays closed for the day. For an app you open out of habit, set a daily open limit and the pause before opening. See the open limit page.

Lock as soon as you lie down

When you get home and want to sleep right away, Quick Focus locks the phone at once. Choose to lock only the apps you pick and set the time to how long you plan to sleep. Calls always work.

If you might undo it when tired

With strict mode on, the unlock code is the only way to loosen rules. In strict mode the code is at least 8 characters with at most 3 successful uses a day, and someone in your family can keep it. Strict mode also turns on Anti-Uninstall. If you forget the code you can request removal, which takes effect after 72 hours.

Adults on iPhone

iPhone v0.1.5 blocks apps by schedule, for example short video during the hours you sleep after a night shift. It can also block deleting apps, protect against clock changes and use an unlock code. It has no daily limits, open limits, pause before opening, Quick Focus, Complete Lock or strict mode.

Kids: at home while a parent works nights

Apps close at bedtime

Before you leave for the night shift, add a bedtime schedule on your child's phone, for example 21:30 to 6:30, with games, short video and cartoon apps on the list. You do not have to worry at work about them playing past midnight.

Cover homework time too

Add another schedule between school and bedtime, for example 18:30 to 20:30, and leave study apps off the list. More on the homework page.

Agree on daily play time in advance

Give games and short video a daily limit, for example 45 minutes a day, after which they stay closed for the day. A game they cannot stop can get a 0 minute limit so it does not open that day.

Kids cannot undo it

Turn on strict mode and keep the unlock code to yourself. With Anti-Uninstall on, your child cannot delete Ripple. Android counts time since boot, so changing the system clock gives no extra time. On Xiaomi, OPPO, vivo, Huawei and Honor phones follow the keep alive guide for auto start and battery settings.

Child on iPhone or iPad

iPhone v0.1.5 blocks apps by schedule, so games and short video stay closed at bedtime. It can block deleting apps, protect against clock changes and use an unlock code, with no daily limits or open limits. See the parental control page.

Setup steps

Install Ripple

On Android download the latest APK and turn on the accessibility service as guided. On iPhone install v0.1.5, which needs iOS 16 or later.

Set up your own phone

Add a sleep schedule based on when your shift ends, one per shift type if you rotate, give short video a daily limit, and use Quick Focus when you lie down.

Set up your child's phone

Before the night shift add bedtime and homework schedules, set daily limits for games and short video, then turn on strict mode and Anti-Uninstall and keep the code yourself.

Check after a week

After a week see whether the schedules and limits fit. When your shift changes, turn on the matching schedule.

FAQ

I scroll instead of sleeping after night shifts. What can I do?

On Android add a sleep schedule that starts when you get home with short video, short dramas and games on the list, give short video a daily limit, and use Quick Focus when you lie down. On iPhone v0.1.5 use schedules.

My shifts rotate. How do I set schedules?

Add one schedule per shift type, turn on the one for this week and pause the others.

Will I miss calls from work while I sleep?

Calls always work. Apps that are not on the list, such as the alarm, phone and messaging apps, are not blocked.

I work nights and my child stays up on the phone. What can I do?

Before you leave, add a bedtime schedule on your child's Android phone or tablet, set daily limits for games and short video, and turn on strict mode and Anti-Uninstall. The apps close on their own.

Can I change the settings from work?

Not at the moment. Ripple's settings stay on that phone and there is no remote management, so set things up on your child's phone in advance.

Does this work on iPhone?

iPhone v0.1.5 blocks selected apps by schedule, can block deleting apps, protects against clock changes and has an unlock code. It has no daily limits, open limits, Quick Focus, Complete Lock or strict mode.

Is it free? Do I need an account?

It is free with no ads and no account. More in the FAQ.
