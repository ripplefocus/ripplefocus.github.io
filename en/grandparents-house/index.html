Scrolling all day at your parents' place, and kids on the phone nonstop at their grandparents'

You only get home a few times a year and you mean to talk with your parents, but after lunch you lie on the sofa and the afternoon goes into your phone while they watch TV next to you. Kids have a similar story. During school holidays they stay with their grandparents, who find it hard to say no, so the phone goes from cartoons to short videos to games all day. This page first covers adults visiting their parents, then what parents can set before kids stay with grandparents. Android is on v0.1.20 and iPhone on v0.1.5, with different features, so they are described separately.

Adults: less phone while you stay with your parents

Keep meals and evenings for family

For the days you are home, add a schedule such as 18:00 to 21:00 every day with short video, short dramas and games on the list, so they stay closed during those hours. Leave WeChat or your chat app and calls off the list so relatives and the family group can still reach you.

A daily allowance for short video

If you still want some scrolling during the day, give short video a daily limit, for example 30 minutes, after which it stays closed for the day. You can also set a daily open limit and the pause before opening. See the open limit page.

Lock a few apps when you go out with them

When you go shopping, walking or visiting relatives with your parents, Quick Focus locks the phone right away. Choose to lock only the apps you pick and set the time to match the trip. Calls always work.

If you might undo it

With strict mode on, the unlock code is the only way to loosen rules. In strict mode the code is at least 8 characters with at most 3 successful uses a day, and your partner or someone in the family can keep it. Strict mode also turns on Anti-Uninstall. If you forget the code you can request removal, which takes effect after 72 hours.

Adults on iPhone

iPhone v0.1.5 blocks apps by schedule, for example short video from dinner until bedtime. It can also block deleting apps, protect against clock changes and use an unlock code. It has no daily limits, open limits, pause before opening, Quick Focus, Complete Lock or strict mode.

Kids: the weeks they stay with grandparents

Set it up before they go

Grandparents may not be comfortable with phone settings, so set things up on your child's Android phone or tablet before dropping them off. Give games and short video a daily limit, for example 60 minutes a day. When it runs out the apps stay closed for the day, so grandparents do not have to argue about it.

Schedules that match the grandparents' routine

Add schedules for meals, nap time and night with games, short video and cartoon apps on the list. For example 21:00 to 7:00 keeps the phone out of bed at night. More on the bedtime page.

A game they should not touch at all

If one game is impossible to stop, set its daily limit to 0 minutes so it does not open at all that day. Leave study apps off the list so homework and online classes keep working.

Begging grandparents will not help

Turn on strict mode and keep the unlock code to yourself without giving it to the grandparents, so no amount of asking can loosen the rules. With Anti-Uninstall on, your child cannot delete Ripple. Android counts time since boot, so changing the system clock gives no extra time. On Xiaomi, OPPO, vivo, Huawei and Honor phones follow the keep alive guide for auto start and battery settings.

Child on iPhone or iPad

iPhone v0.1.5 blocks apps by schedule, so games stay closed at meals and bedtime. It can block deleting apps, protect against clock changes and use an unlock code, with no daily limits or open limits. See the parental control page.

Setup steps

Install Ripple

On Android download the latest APK and turn on the accessibility service as guided. On iPhone install v0.1.5, which needs iOS 16 or later.

Set up your own phone

Before going home, add a schedule from dinner to bedtime, give short video a daily limit, and use Quick Focus when you go out.

Set up your child's phone

Before they stay with grandparents, set daily limits for games and short video and schedules for meals and sleep, then turn on strict mode and Anti-Uninstall and keep the code yourself.

Tell the grandparents

Let them know the apps closing on time is expected, they do not need to unlock anything, and they can call you if something comes up.

FAQ

I spend my whole visit home on my phone. What can I do?

On Android add a schedule from dinner to bedtime with short video and games on the list, and give short video a daily limit for the daytime. On iPhone v0.1.5 use a schedule.

Can my family still reach me?

Leave WeChat or your chat app and calls off the list and they keep working. Calls also work during Quick Focus.

My kids are at their grandparents' and nobody can stop the phone. What can I do?

Before they go, set daily limits for games and short video and a bedtime schedule on your child's Android phone or tablet, and turn on strict mode. The apps close on their own, so grandparents do not have to take the phone away.

Can I change the settings remotely?

Not at the moment. Ripple's settings stay on that phone and there is no remote management, so set things up on your child's phone in advance.

Will my child just use the grandparents' phone?

Ripple only manages the phone it is installed on. If the grandparents use Android you can install Ripple there too, or agree with them not to hand their phone over.

Does this work on iPhone?

iPhone v0.1.5 blocks selected apps by schedule, can block deleting apps, protects against clock changes and has an unlock code. It has no daily limits, open limits, Quick Focus, Complete Lock or strict mode.

Is it free? Do I need an account?

It is free with no ads and no account. More in the FAQ.
