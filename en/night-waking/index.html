Waking up in the night, picking up the phone, and not getting back to sleep

You wake up at two or three in the morning and want to see what time it is. The screen lights up and you open a short video app. You meant to scroll until you felt sleepy, but you only get more awake, and when you put the phone down it is nearly light outside. The next day at work is long. Kids can do the same thing. The phone was supposed to be handed over at 9:30, but once the adults are asleep it goes back under the covers for games and videos, and in the morning nobody can wake them. Ripple can add a schedule from the middle of the night until morning that closes short video and games, while alarms and calls keep working. The bedtime page covers the time before lights out and the baby care page covers scrolling during night feeds. This page covers waking up after you have already fallen asleep. The first part is for adults setting limits on their own phone and the second part is for parents. Android is at v0.1.18 and iPhone is at v0.1.5. They do different things, so each is described separately.

Adults: keep a night waking from turning into an hour of scrolling

Add a schedule from the middle of the night until morning

For example 00:30 to 06:30 every day, with TikTok or Douyin, Kuaishou, Xiaohongshu, Bilibili and the video apps you use most on the list. You can still check the time when you wake up, and short video will not open. If you already have a bedtime schedule, you can move its end time to when you get up.

Keep alarms and calls off the list

Leave the system clock, phone and messages off the list so the morning alarm still rings and family can reach you in an emergency.

Android: close only WeChat Channels and Moments

Many people open WeChat in the night and drift into Channels. On Android you can close only Channels and Moments inside WeChat and keep chats working. See the Channels only page.

Give short video a daily limit too

Heavy scrolling at night often comes with heavy scrolling in the day. On Android you can give the short video app a daily limit in minutes, for example 40 minutes, and it will not open once the time is used up. You can also limit how many times a day it opens. See the open limit page.

Turn on strict mode if you switch rules off at night

Right after waking it is easy to think you will not sleep anyway, so you may as well scroll. With strict mode on, rules can only be loosened with the unlock code. Give a code of at least 8 digits to someone at home. They are asleep in the night, so the rules stay on. An unlock code can succeed at most 3 times a day, and strict mode also turns on Anti-Uninstall. If the code is lost you can request removal after 72 hours. See the strict mode page.

Parents: kids who take the phone back after the adults are asleep

Agree on where the phone stays at night

For example, after 9:30 the phone charges in the living room until morning. When the rule is agreed in advance and the child knows why, it goes better than an argument at midnight.

Add a night schedule on the child's Android phone or tablet

For example 21:30 to 06:30, with games, short video and cartoon apps on the list so they stay closed all night. If the phone is taken back to the bedroom, only things off the list such as calls and the alarm will work.

Changing the system clock does not help

Some kids move the phone clock forward to end the schedule early. Ripple for Android counts time since the phone started, so changing the system clock gives no extra time. See the clock change page.

Strict mode with the unlock code kept by parents

On Android, strict mode means only the unlock code can loosen rules, and it also turns on Anti-Uninstall so the child cannot remove Ripple. Apps on the list are also blocked in split screen, floating windows and picture-in-picture. On OPPO phones a floating window can only be covered and muted and has to be closed by hand. A game the child cannot put down can be set to 0 minutes so it stays closed all day.

If the child uses an iPhone

iPhone v0.1.5 can block apps by schedule, block deleting apps, protect against clock changes and use an unlock code, so you can add a night schedule. Daily limits, 0 minutes, open limits, one-tap focus and strict mode are Android only for now. See the parental control page.

Setup steps (Android v0.1.18)

Install or update to the latest version

Download the latest Android APK and turn on the accessibility service as guided. If you have an older version, update in Settings › About › Check for updates. On iPhone install v0.1.5, which needs iOS 16 or later.

Adult phone: add a 00:30 to 06:30 schedule with short video apps on the list

Keep the clock, phone and messages off the list. In WeChat you can close only Channels and Moments.

Child's phone or tablet: agree the phone charges in the living room, then add a 21:30 to 06:30 schedule

Put games, short video and cartoon apps on the list and keep apps used to contact parents off it.

Set an unlock code and turn on strict mode if needed

An adult's code goes to someone at home and the code on a child's device stays with parents. Strict mode on Android also turns on Anti-Uninstall. On iPhone turn on the block on deleting apps.

FAQ

I wake up in the night, pick up the phone and scroll short videos, and then I cannot sleep. What can I set?

On Ripple for Android v0.1.18 add a schedule from the middle of the night until morning, for example 00:30 to 06:30, with short video apps on the list and the alarm and phone off it. In WeChat you can close only Channels and Moments. If you tend to switch rules off at night, turn on strict mode and give the unlock code to someone at home.

My child takes the phone back after we are asleep. What can I set?

Agree that the phone charges in the living room at night. Then on the child's Android phone or tablet add a 21:30 to 06:30 schedule with games and short video on the list, turn on strict mode with a parent-held unlock code, which also turns on Anti-Uninstall. Changing the system clock does not unlock it. If the child uses an iPhone, block apps by schedule at night.

Will the schedule stop my morning alarm?

No. Only apps on the list are blocked. Keep the system clock and phone off the list and they work as usual.

How is this different from the bedtime, baby care and morning pages?

The bedtime page covers scrolling before lights out, the baby care page covers scrolling during night feeds, and the morning page covers reaching for the phone on waking up. This page covers waking up in the night after you have fallen asleep. See the FAQ for more.

Does the iPhone version have these features?

iPhone is at v0.1.5. It has schedules, the block on deleting apps, clock change protection and an unlock code. It does not have daily limits, open limits, 0 minutes, one-tap focus, strict mode, Channels allowance or website limits.

Does it cost money or need an account?

It is free, has no ads, and needs no account.
