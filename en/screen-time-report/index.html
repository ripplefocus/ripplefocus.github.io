Shocked by 7 or 8 hours a day in your screen time report, and a child's tablet report that is just as high

Many people decide to do something about their phone after seeing the number in the built in Screen Time or Digital Wellbeing page. Seven or eight hours a day, over a hundred pickups, with short video, short dramas and games at the top. Parents who open a child's weekly tablet report often find it well above their guess. Since Android v0.1.19, Ripple also has a usage calendar that keeps records on the phone for 365 days. The iPhone app does not have it. After reading the numbers, you can use Ripple to put the limits in place. This page first covers adults setting limits on their own phone based on their report, then parents doing the same on a child's phone. Android is on v0.1.19 and iPhone on v0.1.5, with different features, so they are covered separately.

Adults: set limits from your own report

Start with the top three apps

Open Screen Time or Digital Wellbeing in the phone's settings and note the three apps you used most last week and roughly how many minutes a day. Start limits with those three and leave the rest for now.

Daily limits for the biggest ones

If short video takes over two hours a day, set it to 60 minutes a day first and drop to 40 after a week. Android daily limits can be any number of minutes from 1 minute to 12 hours, and the app stays closed once the time is used.

Many pickups means an open limit

A high pickup count is usually habit. Give those apps a daily open limit and add the pause before opening. See the open limit page.

Block your heaviest hours

If the report shows most of the time after 11 pm, add a bedtime schedule with those apps on the list so they stay closed. See the bedtime page.

0 minutes for the one you cannot quit

For an app that does little for you and you cannot stop, set 0 minutes so it does not open all day. If you might change it back, turn on strict mode, which makes the unlock code the only way to loosen rules. In strict mode the code is at least 8 characters with at most 3 successful uses a day, and a family member or friend can keep it. Strict mode also turns on Anti-Uninstall. If you forget the code you can request removal, which takes effect after 72 hours.

Adults on iPhone

iPhone v0.1.5 blocks apps by schedule, for example during the evening hours your report shows as heaviest. It can also block deleting apps, protect against clock changes and use an unlock code. It has no daily limits, open limits, pause before opening, Quick Focus, Complete Lock or strict mode.

Kids: set limits from your child's report

Look at the numbers together

Open the weekly usage on your child's tablet or phone and go through which apps took the time together. Kids accept limits more easily after seeing the numbers themselves.

Daily limits for games and short video

If your child plays two hours a day, set 40 minutes a day to start and agree on weekends separately. Once the time is used the app stays closed for the day.

Block homework and sleep time

If the report shows heavy use during homework and after 9 pm, add a homework schedule and a sleep schedule with games and short video on the list. See the homework page.

Kids cannot undo it

Turn on strict mode and keep the unlock code to yourself. With Anti-Uninstall on, your child cannot delete Ripple. Android counts time since boot, so changing the system clock gives no extra time. On Xiaomi, OPPO, vivo, Huawei and Honor phones follow the keep alive guide for auto start and battery settings.

Child on iPhone or iPad

iPhone v0.1.5 blocks apps by schedule, so games and short video stay closed during homework and sleep time. It can block deleting apps, protect against clock changes and use an unlock code, with no daily limits or open limits. Ripple has no remote management, so set it up on the child's device. See the parental control page.

Setup steps

Read one week of the report

In Screen Time or Digital Wellbeing, note the three most used apps and the hours with the most use.

Install Ripple

On Android download the latest APK and turn on the accessibility service as guided. On iPhone install v0.1.5, which needs iOS 16 or later.

Set limits from the report

Daily limits for heavy apps, open limits for apps with many pickups, schedules for the heaviest hours. On a child's phone also turn on strict mode and Anti-Uninstall and keep the code yourself.

Check the report after a week

Look at screen time again after a week. Keep the limits if the number dropped, and shorten them a bit if it did not drop enough.

FAQ

My screen time is 7 or 8 hours a day. Where do I start?

Take the three most used apps and give them daily limits a bit below what you use now, then lower them after a week. Add open limits for apps with many pickups and schedules for your heaviest hours. On iPhone v0.1.5 use schedules.

Does Ripple show usage statistics?

On Android, yes, from v0.1.19. The "Time used today" card on the Today page opens a usage calendar with each day's total, time per app, open counts and block counts. Records stay on the phone for 365 days. Ripple for iPhone v0.1.5 does not have it, so use the built in Screen Time there.

What if I set limits and then undo them?

On Android turn on strict mode. After that the unlock code is the only way to loosen rules, a family member or friend can keep it, and Anti-Uninstall turns on too.

My child's weekly tablet report is high. What can I do?

On your child's Android phone or tablet give games and short video daily limits, add schedules for homework and sleep, and turn on strict mode and Anti-Uninstall with the code kept by you.

Can I set it up remotely from my own phone?

Not at the moment. Ripple's settings stay on that phone and there is no remote management, so set it up on your child's phone directly.

Does this work on iPhone?

iPhone v0.1.5 blocks selected apps by schedule, can block deleting apps, protects against clock changes and has an unlock code. It has no daily limits, open limits, Quick Focus, Complete Lock or strict mode.

Is it free? Do I need an account?

It is free with no ads and no account. More in the FAQ.
