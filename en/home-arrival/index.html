Scrolling on the sofa from the moment you get home until dinner

After a day at work you open the door, drop your bag, and lie down on the sofa with your shoes still on. You open a short video app for a few minutes of rest. When you look up it is dinner time, someone has called you several times, and the cooking and tidying you meant to do have not started. Kids coming home from school do much the same. The school bag goes on the floor and they look for the phone or tablet. They promise to play for a bit and then do homework, and the homework gets pushed to nine or ten at night. Ripple can switch off short video and games for the stretch between getting home and dinner, and it can also give those apps a daily allowance. The after dinner page covers the evening after the meal, the homework page covers parents scrolling while kids do homework, and the commute page covers the trip home. This page is only about the time from walking in the door until dinner. The first part is for adults setting limits for themselves, and the second part is for parents. Android is at v0.1.20 and iPhone at v0.1.5. They can do different things, so each is described separately.

Adults: walk in the door without lying down to scroll

A schedule from getting home to dinner

For example, every day from 17:30 to 19:00, put TikTok or Douyin, Kuaishou, Xiaohongshu and Bilibili on the list so they cannot open. Change your clothes, start dinner or talk with your family. Keep calls, WeChat and food delivery apps off the list. Outside the schedule the apps work as usual.

A daily limit on the short video app

If you do not want to block the whole stretch, give only the short video app a daily limit in minutes, for example 40 minutes. Time on the way home and at home counts together. When it runs out the app stays closed until the next day.

An open limit and a short wait before opening

At home you pick up the phone, put it down, and pick it up again. Turn on the short wait before each open for short video apps and set how many times a day they can be opened. When the opens run out, the app stays closed for the day. See the open limit page.

One-tap focus when you want to get things done first

If you want to get dinner going or finish something from the day, start a focus session from the home screen with one tap and pick a length. Short video and games stay closed until it ends. See the one-tap focus page.

Strict mode if you switch rules off when tired

The end of a work day is when it is easiest to decide today does not count. With strict mode, rules can only be relaxed with an unlock code. Give a code of at least 8 digits to someone at home. An unlock code can succeed at most 3 times a day. Strict mode also turns on Anti-Uninstall. If the code is lost you can request removal, which takes effect after 72 hours. See the strict mode page.

Parents: the child wants the phone first and homework waits until late

Agree on the order after school

Have a snack and a rest first, then homework, then the phone, and say how long the phone can be used. When you set the rules together, kids accept them more easily and there are fewer arguments later.

An after school schedule on the child's Android phone or tablet

For example, from 16:30 to 19:30 put games, short video and cartoon apps on the list, so they stay closed from getting home until homework is done. If homework is finished early, a parent can enter the unlock code to open things for a while. Keep calls, WeChat and study apps off the list.

Set the game they cannot put down to 0 minutes

If one game is especially hard to stop, set it to 0 minutes so it stays closed all day. Give other games and short video a daily limit, for example 30 minutes on school days. See the mobile games page.

Strict mode with the code kept by family

With strict mode on Android, only the unlock code can relax the rules, and Anti-Uninstall is turned on so the child cannot remove Ripple. A parent or another adult at home keeps the code. Listed apps are also blocked in split screen, floating windows and picture-in-picture. On OPPO phones a floating window can only be covered and muted, and the window itself has to be closed by hand. Changing the system clock does not add time.

If the child uses an iPhone

Ripple for iPhone v0.1.5 can block apps by schedule, block deleting apps, resist clock changes and use an unlock code, so you can add a schedule from after school until homework is done. Daily limits, 0 minutes, open limits, one-tap focus and strict mode are Android only for now. See the parental control page.

Setup steps (Android v0.1.20)

Install or update to the latest version

On Android, download the latest APK and turn on the accessibility service as guided. If you have an older version, update in Settings › About › Check for updates. On iPhone install v0.1.5, which needs iOS 16 or later.

Adult phone: add a 17:30 to 19:00 schedule, or give the short video app a daily limit and an open limit

Keep calls, WeChat, maps and food delivery apps off the list.

Child's phone or tablet: agree on the order, then add a schedule from after school until homework is done, and set the hardest game to 0 minutes

Keep the apps used to reach parents and study apps off the list.

Set an unlock code and turn on strict mode if needed

Adults give their code to someone at home, and parents keep the code for the child's device. Strict mode on Android also turns on Anti-Uninstall. On iPhone, turn on the block on deleting apps.

FAQ

I get home from work, lie on the sofa and scroll short videos until dinner. What can I set?

On Ripple for Android v0.1.20, add a schedule from 17:30 to 19:00 with short video apps on the list, or give the short video app a daily limit and an open limit. Use one-tap focus when you want to get things done first. If you switch rules off when tired, turn on strict mode and give the unlock code to someone at home.

My child asks for the phone as soon as they get home and homework waits until late. What can I set?

Agree with your child that homework comes before the phone. Then on the child's Android phone or tablet add a schedule from after school until homework is done, for example 16:30 to 19:30, with games and short video on the list, and set the hardest game to 0 minutes. Turn on strict mode and keep the unlock code. If the child uses an iPhone, block apps by schedule after school.

What if homework is done and my child can play for a while?

A parent enters the unlock code to open things for a while. An unlock code can succeed at most 3 times a day, and a wrong code or a cancel does not count.

How is this different from the after dinner, homework and commute pages?

The after dinner page covers the evening after the meal, the homework page covers parents scrolling while kids do homework, and the commute page covers the trip home. This page covers the time from getting home until dinner. For weekends see the weekend page, and see the FAQ for more.

Does the iPhone version have these features?

iPhone is at v0.1.5. It has schedules, the block on deleting apps, clock change protection and an unlock code. It does not have daily limits, open limits, 0 minutes, one-tap focus, strict mode, Channels allowance or website limits.

Does it cost money or need an account?

It is free, has no ads, and needs no account.
