On your phone all summer at home, and kids on the phone all summer break

Before the break you plan to read a few books and learn something. Once it starts you get up at noon, pick up the phone, and a month later none of the plan has happened. It happens to college students back home, teachers on break and anyone on a long leave. Kids are harder. Summer break lasts about two months, parents are at work during the day, and a child alone at home does a bit of homework and then spends the afternoon on the phone. This page first covers adults planning their own break, then what parents can set while they are at work. Android is on v0.1.19 and iPhone on v0.1.5, with different features, so they are described separately.

Adults: the weeks you are off at home

Keep mornings for what you planned

Add a schedule such as 9:00 to 12:00 every day with short video, short dramas and games on the list, so they stay closed during those hours. Leave reading, vocabulary and exam prep apps off the list. More on the exam season page.

Set an amount for scrolling

If you still want some scrolling, give short video a daily limit, for example 40 minutes, after which it stays closed for the day. For an app you open dozens of times a day, set a daily open limit and the pause before opening. See the open limit page.

Stop scrolling until 3 a.m.

Late nights are the easiest habit on a break. A schedule from 23:30 to 7:30 keeps short video and games closed overnight and helps your sleep come back. More on the bedtime page.

Lock right away when you sit down to study

When you want to focus on a book for a while, Quick Focus locks the phone right away. Choose to lock only the apps you pick and set the time yourself. Calls always work.

If you might undo it

With strict mode on, the unlock code is the only way to loosen rules. In strict mode the code is at least 8 characters with at most 3 successful uses a day, and someone in your family or a friend can keep it. Strict mode also turns on Anti-Uninstall. If you forget the code you can request removal, which takes effect after 72 hours.

Adults on iPhone

iPhone v0.1.5 blocks apps by schedule, for example short video in the morning and before bed. It can also block deleting apps, protect against clock changes and use an unlock code. It has no daily limits, open limits, pause before opening, Quick Focus, Complete Lock or strict mode.

Kids: home alone while parents are at work

Schedules that follow a summer routine

Agree on a summer routine with your child, such as homework in the morning and sport or reading in the afternoon. Add a schedule from 8:30 to 11:30 with games, short video and cartoon apps on the list, and leave study apps and online classes off it. More on the homework page.

Agree on daily play time in advance

Give games and short video a daily limit, for example 60 minutes a day. When it runs out the apps stay closed for the day, so you do not have to call home from work to remind them.

A game that stays off

If one game is impossible to stop, set its daily limit to 0 minutes so it does not open at all that day. Change it back once the summer homework is done.

Kids cannot undo it

Turn on strict mode and keep the unlock code to yourself. With Anti-Uninstall on, your child cannot delete Ripple. Android counts time since boot, so changing the system clock gives no extra time. On Xiaomi, OPPO, vivo, Huawei and Honor phones follow the keep alive guide for auto start and battery settings.

Child on iPhone or iPad

iPhone v0.1.5 blocks apps by schedule, so games stay closed in the morning and at bedtime. It can block deleting apps, protect against clock changes and use an unlock code, with no daily limits or open limits. See the parental control page.

Setup steps

Install Ripple

On Android download the latest APK and turn on the accessibility service as guided. On iPhone install v0.1.5, which needs iOS 16 or later.

Set up your own phone

On the first day of the break add morning and bedtime schedules, give short video a daily limit, and use Quick Focus when you study.

Set up your child's phone

Agree on a summer routine, add schedules that follow it, set daily limits for games and short video, then turn on strict mode and Anti-Uninstall and keep the code yourself.

Check after a week

After a week see whether the limits fit and adjust them together with your child. Finished homework can earn a little more time.

FAQ

I am on my phone all day during the summer. What can I do?

On Android add a morning schedule and a bedtime schedule with short video and games on the list, and give short video a daily limit. On iPhone v0.1.5 use schedules.

Will my study apps get blocked?

Only apps on the list are blocked. Reading, vocabulary and online class apps keep working.

I work during the day and my child is on the phone at home. What can I do?

On your child's Android phone or tablet add schedules that follow the summer routine, set daily limits for games and short video, and turn on strict mode. The apps close on their own even when you are not home.

Can I change the settings from work?

Not at the moment. Ripple's settings stay on that phone and there is no remote management, so set things up on your child's phone in advance.

Will my child use another device at home?

Ripple only manages the phone or tablet it is installed on. If an old phone or tablet at home runs Android, you can install Ripple on it too.

Does this work on iPhone?

iPhone v0.1.5 blocks selected apps by schedule, can block deleting apps, protects against clock changes and has an unlock code. It has no daily limits, open limits, Quick Focus, Complete Lock or strict mode.

Is it free? Do I need an account?

It is free with no ads and no account. More in the FAQ.
