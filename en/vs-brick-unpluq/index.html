Ripple vs Brick and Unpluq

Brick and Unpluq add something you can hold. You block apps in their app, and to get the apps back you have to tap your phone on a small NFC device, so it helps to leave that device in another room. Ripple does not use any hardware. The friction comes from a wait, a daily amount and an unlock code that someone else can keep. I make Ripple, so read this with that in mind. Ripple for Android is v0.1.20 and Ripple for iPhone is v0.1.5. Facts about Brick and Unpluq come from their own websites and help pages in November 2026, so check their latest pages before you buy.

What each one is

Brick

A small battery free NFC puck. Brick lists it at $59 with no subscription. You pick apps or websites to block in the Brick app, tap the phone on the puck to start, and tap again to stop. Its site says it works on Android 12 or later with NFC and on iOS 18 or later. One puck works with any number of phones, and there are five emergency unlocks if you are away from it.

Unpluq

An app with a keychain NFC tag. Its FAQ says the free version blocks two apps on one schedule. More apps, more schedules and the tag need Unpluq Premium, which is paid. During a schedule you open a blocked app by scanning the tag or by another barrier such as shaking the phone. A subscription covers one phone and one tablet.

Ripple

A free app with no ads and no account, and no hardware. On Android (8.0 or later) it has schedules, a daily amount per app including 0 minutes, open limits, a break rule, quick focus and strict mode. On iPhone v0.1.5 it only blocks by schedule, stops apps being deleted, protects the clock and uses an unlock code.

For your own phone

If a walk to another room is what works for you

That is the real strength of Brick and Unpluq. Ripple has nothing physical. If you know you will type any code you know by heart, a device you leave at home may suit you better.

If you want a daily amount instead of on and off

Ripple for Android gives each app its own daily amount, such as 30 minutes of short video, and shows the block screen once it is used up. Open limits cap how many times a day you open an app.

A wait before you can loosen a rule

Without strict mode, loosening a rule in Ripple needs a 15 minute wait (30 minutes for apps like Douyin) or the unlock code. With strict mode only the code works, at most 3 successful uses a day. You can ask a friend to set the code and keep it, which plays a similar part to leaving a puck in another room.

Only part of WeChat

Ripple for Android can block only WeChat Channels or Moments and leave chats and calls alone. I have not seen this in Brick or Unpluq.

On iPhone

Brick and Unpluq do more on iPhone than Ripple v0.1.5 does. Ripple for iPhone has schedules, the unlock code, clock protection and blocking app deletion, and no daily amounts.

For a child's phone

A puck the child can walk to

A physical device in the house only works for a child if it is somewhere the child cannot reach. Brick mentions use at home and in classrooms. Think about where it would live before you buy one.

Per app limits on Android

On a child's Android phone Ripple can give the game, cartoon and short video apps their own daily amounts, set one game to 0 minutes so it stays closed all day, and add homework and bedtime schedules.

Rules the child cannot undo

Turn on strict mode and keep the code yourself. Anti-Uninstall turns on with strict mode and needs Android 11 or later. Changing the clock does not end a schedule. A forgotten code is lifted 72 hours after a request.

No remote management

Ripple has no parent app and no remote control, so rules are set on the child's phone. Unpluq has a separate Family product, and Google Family Link or Apple Screen Time with Family Sharing let you manage from your own phone.

Child on iPhone or iPad

Ripple for iPhone v0.1.5 can block apps by schedule on the child's device with a code you keep. For daily amounts use Screen Time with Family Sharing.

How to choose

Pick Brick

If you want a one time purchase, a physical step to unlock, and your phone is Android 12 or later with NFC or iOS 18 or later.

Pick Unpluq

If you like a tag on your keys plus other unlock barriers, and you are fine with a paid subscription for more than two apps.

Pick Ripple

If you are on Android and want free per app daily amounts, open limits, strict mode and WeChat Channels or Moments blocking, without buying hardware or making an account.

Try it

Install the latest APK, turn on the accessibility service when asked, add one app with a 30 minute daily amount and open it to check the block screen shows. If the phone cleans up background apps, follow the keep alive guide.

FAQ

Does Ripple need an NFC tag or any hardware?

No. Ripple is only an app. The unlock code takes the place of a device, and someone else can keep it.

Is Ripple really free?

Yes. No ads, no subscription and no account. More in the FAQ.

Can I use Brick and Ripple together?

They are separate apps, and I have not tested them together on one phone, so I cannot say how they behave side by side.

Can I change my child's Ripple rules from my phone?

No. Ripple has no remote management. Rules are set on the child's phone.

What does Ripple for iPhone do?

Ripple for iPhone v0.1.5 blocks apps by schedule, can stop apps being deleted, protects against clock changes and uses an unlock code. It has no daily limits.
