Less scrolling in bed when you stay at a hotel

On a work trip you get back to the room after the meetings, take a shower and lie down. Nobody is around and there is nothing to do, so one short video follows another, and when you look up it is after two, with an early meeting the next morning. Family trips go the same way. In a hotel or guesthouse the first thing a child asks for is the Wi-Fi password, and after that it is cartoons and games on the tablet, still going at eleven, while the parents are too tired to deal with it. The routine from home falls apart once you leave, and phones get used more than usual. Ripple lets you take your home rules on the road. Short video, short drama and games close at the set times. Settings stay on the phone and need no account, so nothing has to be set up again on the hotel network. The site has a travel page about long car rides and an airport and train page about waiting to board. This page is about the evenings once you reach the place you are staying. The first part below is for adults limiting their own phone, the second is for parents setting up a child's tablet or phone. Android is at v0.1.19 and iPhone at v0.1.5, and the features differ, so both are covered.

Adults: less scrolling alone in a hotel room

Add a night schedule before you leave

At home someone may tell you to go to sleep. In a hotel it is just you. Before the trip, add a schedule from 11:30 pm to 7 am with short video, short drama and games on the list. They will not open during it and come back in the morning. Leave calls, WeChat, maps, ride hailing and the alarm off the list, so you can still reach colleagues, get a car and set an alarm. See the bedtime page.

Set daily limits on short video and short drama

Evenings on a trip have a lot of free time, and a whole short drama series goes by easily. Give these apps a daily limit in custom minutes, for example 40 minutes a day. When it runs out they will not open that day. See the short drama page.

Add a short wait before each open and an open limit

Picking up the phone in bed is almost a reflex. Turn on the short wait before each open for short video apps and set how many times a day they can open. In those few seconds you sometimes remember the early start tomorrow. See the open limit page.

Use one-tap focus when you have work to finish

If you still need to fix slides or answer email in the hotel at night, tap once on the home screen to start focus right away. Short video and games will not open until it ends. See the one-tap focus page.

Turn on strict mode if you switch rules off on the road

Alone on a trip, nobody would know if you switched the rules off. With strict mode on, loosening the rules needs the unlock code. Give an unlock code of at least 8 digits to someone at home. Strict mode turns on Anti-Uninstall too, and a lost code can be removed after a 72 hour wait. See the strict mode page.

Parents: a child on the hotel Wi-Fi who will not put the tablet down

Agree on the rules before you go

Holidays can be a little looser than usual, and it helps to say so up front. Agree with your child before the trip on how long they can watch each day and when the tablet goes down at night, then set that in Ripple. In the hotel you will not need to argue about it every evening.

Set daily limits on the child's tablet or old phone

On the Android tablet or old phone your child takes along, give games and short video a daily limit. When it runs out they will not open. Hotel Wi-Fi makes no difference, because the rules live on the device. A game that is especially hard to put down can be set to 0 minutes for the days you are away. See the mobile games page.

Add a bedtime schedule

Add a schedule on the child's device from 9:30 pm to 7 am with games, short video and cartoon apps on the list. After a full day out, bedtime is easier to keep. Keep calls, WeChat and the apps used to reach parents off the list, so the child can always find you if you get separated.

Split screen, floating windows and clock changes

On Android, listed apps are blocked in split screen, floating windows and picture-in-picture. On OPPO phones a floating window can only be covered and muted and has to be closed by hand. Ripple for Android counts time since the phone started, so moving the clock forward or back does not add time. See the clock change page.

Parents keep the unlock code; iPhone

Only parents know the unlock code. Turn on Anti-Uninstall on Android so the child cannot delete Ripple. iPhone v0.1.5 has schedules, the block on deleting apps, clock change protection and an unlock code, so the bedtime schedule works there too. Daily limits, 0 minutes, open limits, one-tap focus and strict mode are Android only for now. See the parental control page.

Setup steps (Android v0.1.19)

Install or update to the latest version before the trip

Download the latest Android APK and turn on the Accessibility service as guided. If you have an older version, update in Settings › About › Check for updates. On iPhone install v0.1.5, which needs iOS 16 or later. It is easiest to set things up at home.

Adult phone: a night schedule, plus daily limits and open limits on short video and short drama

Keep calls, WeChat, maps, ride hailing and the alarm off the list.

Child's tablet or old phone: agree with the child first, then set daily limits or 0 minutes on games and short video and add a bedtime schedule

Keep the apps used to reach parents off the list.

Set an unlock code and turn on Anti-Uninstall on the child's device

Adults give their unlock code to someone at home. Parents keep the code for the child's device. Turn on Anti-Uninstall on Android, and strict mode if you want it firmer. On iPhone turn on the block on deleting apps.

FAQ

On work trips I scroll short videos in the hotel until the early hours. What can I set?

On Ripple for Android v0.1.19, add a night schedule before you leave with short video, short drama and games on the list, then set daily limits and open limits and turn on the short wait before each open. If you tend to switch rules off, turn on strict mode and give the unlock code to someone at home. Keep calls, WeChat, maps and the alarm off the list.

On a family trip my child stays on the tablet over hotel Wi-Fi and will not sleep. What can I set?

Agree with your child before the trip on daily time and bedtime. Then on the child's Android tablet or old phone, set daily limits on games and short video, or 0 minutes for the trip, and add a bedtime schedule. The rules live on the device, so a different network changes nothing. Parents keep the unlock code and turn on Anti-Uninstall.

Do I need to set things up again in a new place, or be online?

No. Ripple keeps its settings on the phone and needs no account, so the rules are still there on hotel or guesthouse Wi-Fi.

How is this different from the travel and airport pages?

The travel page covers long car rides and the airport and train page covers waiting to board. This page covers evenings after you check in. See the FAQ for more.

Does the iPhone version have these features?

iPhone is at v0.1.5. It has schedules, the block on deleting apps, clock change protection and an unlock code. It does not have daily limits, open limits, 0 minutes, one-tap focus, strict mode, Channels allowance or website limits.

Does it cost money or need an account?

It is free, has no ads, and needs no account.
