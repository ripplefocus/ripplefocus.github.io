Ripple vs OurPact and Norton Family

OurPact and Norton Family are parental control services. A parent signs in, pairs the child's phone, and changes rules from their own phone or a website. Ripple works on one phone with no account, so it suits a parent who can hold the child's phone to set things up, and an adult who wants limits on their own phone. I make Ripple, so read this with that in mind. Ripple for Android is v0.1.22 and Ripple for iPhone is v0.1.5. Facts about OurPact come from ourpact.com, its help pages and the US App Store, and facts about Norton Family from norton.com and its support pages, all in November 2026. Prices change and vary by country, so check before you pay.

What each one is

OurPact

Parent apps for iPhone and Android and a website. It manages a child's iPhone, iPad, Android phone or tablet. Premium is $6.99 a month or $69.99 a year in the US and covers up to 20 child devices: block schedules, instant blocks, a daily screen time allowance, app rules (always blocked, always allowed or blocked by schedule), web restrictions, blocking texting, new app alerts, removal alerts and live location with 7 days of history. Premium+ at $9.99 a month or $99.99 a year adds screenshots of the child's screen and 30 days of location history. Both have a 14 day trial. The free tier manages one device with five manual blocks a month and one schedule. Its help page says it does not work on Xiaomi, Motorola, TCL, Honor, OnePlus, Oppo, Tecno or ZTE phones.

Norton Family

A parental control subscription from Norton, $49.99 for the first year in the US, with a 30 day trial. It runs on a child's Windows PC, Android phone or tablet, iPhone and iPad, and parents use a website or the mobile app. It shows the sites and search terms a child uses and blocks unsuitable sites, sets allowed and blocked hours, and sends activity reports and alerts. On Android and Windows it can block chosen apps and set a daily device time limit. On iPhone its support pages say there is no daily time limit, only blocked hours, and app supervision is not available.

Ripple

A free app with no ads, no account and no subscription. On Android (8.0 or later) it has schedules, a daily amount per app including 0 minutes, open limits, a break rule, quick focus, blocked websites, a usage calendar, a mode that closes only WeChat Channels and Moments, and strict mode. On iPhone v0.1.5 it only blocks by schedule, stops apps being deleted, protects the clock and uses an unlock code. It has no parent app, no location and no reports.

For a child's phone

Changing rules from your own phone

OurPact and Norton Family let you do this, which helps with teens who are out a lot. Ripple cannot. Its rules live on the child's phone and you need the phone in your hand to change them.

A different limit for each app

In Ripple every app gets its own daily amount, for example 30 minutes of a game and 0 minutes of a short video app. OurPact uses one allowance with app rules on top. Norton Family limits time for the whole device.

Seeing what the child does

Norton Family shows sites and searches, and OurPact Premium+ takes screenshots. Ripple shows nothing to anyone. If you want monitoring, Ripple is the wrong tool, and some families prefer it for that reason.

Rules the child cannot undo

Turn on strict mode in Ripple and keep the unlock code yourself. Anti-Uninstall turns on with strict mode and needs Android 11 or later. Changing the clock does not end a schedule. A forgotten code is lifted 72 hours after a request. OurPact sends a removal alert if its app is removed.

Xiaomi, Oppo, Honor or OnePlus phones

OurPact says it does not support these brands. Ripple runs on them, though some of them close apps in the background, so follow the keep alive guide after installing.

Child on iPhone or iPad

Ripple for iPhone v0.1.5 can only block apps by schedule with a code you keep. OurPact covers iPhone more fully. Screen Time with Family Sharing is free and built in.

For your own phone

Limits on your own phone

OurPact and Norton Family are built around a parent account managing a child's device. Ripple is set up on the phone you use, so you can give yourself 20 minutes of Instagram a day or block a game on weeknights.

Making the limit stick

In Ripple, loosening a rule or removing the app needs a 15 minute wait or the unlock code. Loosening a rule on apps like Douyin and Kuaishou waits 30 minutes. With strict mode only the code works, at most 3 successful uses a day, and a partner or friend can keep it.

Paying nothing

Ripple is free with nothing to unlock. OurPact and Norton Family are subscriptions after the trial, apart from OurPact's small free tier.

How to choose

Choose OurPact

You want to manage a child's phone remotely with schedules, app rules and location, and the child's phone is not one of the brands it skips.

Choose Norton Family

You mainly care about web filtering and seeing searches, maybe across a Windows PC as well as a phone.

Choose Ripple

You want free per app limits on Android with no account and nothing sent off the phone, for yourself or for a child whose phone you can set up in person.

Try it first

Install the latest APK, turn on the accessibility service when asked, give one app 30 minutes a day and open it to see the block screen.

FAQ

Can I change Ripple rules on my child's phone from my phone?

No. Ripple has no parent app and no account. You change rules on the child's phone, and with strict mode on you need the unlock code.

Does Ripple track location or take screenshots?

No. Ripple has no location, no screenshots and no reports. Settings stay on the phone.

Is Ripple really free?

Yes. No ads, no subscription and no account. More in the FAQ.

What does the iPhone version do?

Ripple for iPhone v0.1.5 blocks apps by schedule, blocks deleting apps, protects the clock and uses an unlock code. It has no daily amounts.
