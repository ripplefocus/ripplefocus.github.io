Video apps that pay coins for watching

Some short video and news apps come in a Lite edition that pays coins for watching, for checking in every day and for inviting friends. TikTok Lite has run rewards like this in some countries, and in China the Lite editions of Douyin, Kuaishou, Toutiao and Xigua are built around them. The coins make the time feel useful, so it is easy to keep watching for one more reward. Adults get stuck on the daily tasks. Kids find the apps on their own or on a grandparent's phone and like seeing a balance go up. I make Ripple, a free app blocker. Ripple for Android is v0.1.19 and Ripple for iPhone is v0.1.5. The first part is for adults limiting their own phone and the second part is for parents.

Adults: the daily coin tasks

Add the Lite edition as its own app

A Lite edition is a separate app with its own icon. If you limit the main app and forget the Lite one, the time just moves over. Add both to the same daily limit or schedule.

Give them one daily limit

Put the reward apps on a daily limit, for example 30 minutes. When the time is used up the app shows the block screen until the next day. A 0 minute limit blocks an app for the whole day.

Fewer quick check ins

Reward apps send you back for small tasks many times a day. A daily open limit and a pause before opening help here. Each open waits 5 to 30 seconds and shows how long you have used the app today. See open limits.

Turn off the reminders

Ripple does not manage notifications. Turn off notifications for the reward apps in your phone settings so the check in reminders stop.

Look at the week

The usage calendar on Android shows each day's total and each app's time, opens and blocks. Put the hours from the week next to what the coins were worth and decide if it is worth keeping the app.

When you keep loosening it

Without strict mode, loosening a rule needs a wait of 15 minutes by default, up to 2 hours, or the unlock code. With strict mode on only the code works, with at most 3 successful uses a day. Strict mode also turns on Anti-Uninstall, which needs Android 11 or later.

Parents: kids collecting coins

Check which apps are on the phone

Look through the child's phone for Lite editions and other apps that pay for watching. They are easy to miss because the names look like the main apps.

Schedules for homework and nights

Add a homework schedule and a school night schedule such as 21:00 to 7:00 with the reward apps, games and short video apps on the list. A 0 minute limit keeps one app closed all day.

Ask about the account

Reward apps often ask for a phone number or a payment account to cash out coins. Talk with your child about whose account it is, and keep payment passwords to yourself.

Kids who know the settings

Turn on strict mode and keep the unlock code yourself. Anti-Uninstall turns on with it. Time is counted from device uptime, so changing the clock does not end a schedule. A forgotten code can be requested and is lifted after 72 hours.

Grandparents' phones

If the child uses a grandparent's phone, the rules have to be set on that phone too. Ripple rules stay on the device where you set them and there is no remote management.

Child on iPhone

Ripple for iPhone v0.1.5 blocks apps by schedule and can stop apps being deleted, protect against clock changes and use an unlock code. It has no daily limits, open limits or strict mode, so use Screen Time with Family Sharing for amounts.

Setup steps

Install

On Android download the latest APK and turn on the accessibility service when asked. Ripple needs Android 8.0 or later. If you already have it, update in Settings › About › Check for updates.

Keep it running

Some phones stop background apps. Follow the keep alive guide for your brand so blocks stay on.

Adults: limits on main and Lite apps

Put the main and Lite editions on one daily limit, add an open limit, and turn on strict mode only if you keep loosening the rules.

Parents: lists and strict mode

Add homework and night schedules with the reward apps, then turn on strict mode and keep the code.

On iPhone

In Ripple for iPhone pick the reward apps for a schedule. Use Screen Time in Settings for daily amounts.

FAQ

Does limiting the main app also limit the Lite edition?

No. They are separate apps, so add each one to the schedule or daily limit.

Can Ripple stop the coin notifications?

No. Turn off notifications for those apps in your phone settings.

Can I check my child's phone from my own phone?

No. Ripple has no remote management. Check the usage calendar on the child's Android phone together.

What does Ripple for iPhone do?

Ripple for iPhone v0.1.5 blocks apps by schedule, can stop apps being deleted, protects against clock changes and uses an unlock code. It has no daily limits.

Does it cost anything?

Ripple is free, with no ads and no account. More in the FAQ.
