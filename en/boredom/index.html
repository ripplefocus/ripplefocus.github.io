Picking up the phone whenever you are bored, and kids asking for it the moment they say I'm bored

When the hands have nothing to do, they go to the phone. Waiting for the kettle, sitting on the sofa after dinner, lying in bed before sleep. You open a short video app for a quick look, and when you look up a lot of time has gone. Often you did not want to watch anything in particular, you were just a little bored. Kids do the same. Homework is done, Saturday afternoon has no plan, they say I'm bored and ask for the phone or tablet. Ripple can make you wait a few seconds before a distracting app opens, and it can cap how many times a day you open it and how long you use it. The first part is for adults setting this up for themselves and the second part is for parents. Android is on v0.1.17 and iPhone on v0.1.5, with different features, so they are described separately.

Adults: when you have a spare moment, do not go straight to short videos

Wait a few seconds before it opens

On Android turn on the pause before opening for the short video app you open most. During the pause the screen shows how many times you opened it today and how long you used it. Bored opens are usually automatic, and seeing that number is often enough to close it. See the open limit page.

Cap opens per day

Also cap how many times that app can open per day, for example 5. Once the opens are used up it stays closed for the day, so the quick look you take whenever you are idle happens far less.

Cap the total time

Give short video and short drama apps a daily limit in minutes, for example 30 minutes a day, and they stay closed once it is used up. The one you can never put down can be set to 0 minutes so it does not open all day.

The time of day you get bored most

If the dull stretch is usually from dinner to bed, add a schedule such as 21:00 to 23:00, and listed apps close at that time. On a day you want to rein yourself in right away, tap Quick Focus and pick a length.

If you tend to undo your rules

Turn on strict mode on Android. Rules can then only be loosened with the unlock code. In strict mode the code is at least 8 characters and works at most 3 times a day. Give it to a family member or friend. Strict mode turns on Anti-Uninstall too, and if you forget the code you can request removal, which takes effect after 72 hours.

If you use an iPhone

iPhone v0.1.5 blocks apps by schedule, so you can add a schedule for the hours you most often scroll out of boredom and short videos will not open then. It can also block deleting apps, protect against clock changes and use an unlock code. It has no pause before opening, open limits, daily limits, Quick Focus or strict mode.

Parents: the child says I'm bored and asks for the phone

Agree how long they can play each day

Agree with your child on daily time for games and short videos, for example 40 minutes, and give those apps a daily limit. Once it is used up they stay closed for the day, so the next I'm bored does not start a new negotiation. A game you do not want them to play can be set to 0 minutes.

Cap how often they open it

Bored kids tend to open an app again and again. Give the apps they use most a daily open limit and turn on the pause before opening, so each time they see how many times they already opened it today.

Empty hours on weekends and holidays

Weekend afternoons and school holiday days are when a whole block of time goes to the phone. Add a schedule such as Saturday and Sunday 14:00 to 17:00, and games and short videos close then, leaving that time for going out, reading or other things. See the weekend page.

The child cannot change it

On Android with strict mode, rules can only be loosened with the unlock code, which the parent keeps. With Anti-Uninstall on, the child cannot remove Ripple. Ripple on Android counts time since boot, so changing the system clock does not add time. Listed apps are also blocked in split screen, floating windows and PiP. On OPPO phones a floating window can only be covered and muted, and it has to be closed by hand.

Keep alive settings on Chinese brand phones

Xiaomi, OPPO, vivo, Huawei and Honor phones may stop Ripple in the background. After installing, follow the keep alive guide to set autostart and battery optimization.

If the child uses an iPhone or iPad

iPhone v0.1.5 blocks apps by schedule, so games and short videos do not open during empty stretches such as weekend afternoons. It can also block deleting apps, protect against clock changes and use an unlock code. It has no daily limits or open limits. See the parental control page.

Setup steps

Install Ripple

On Android download the latest APK and turn on the accessibility service as guided. On iPhone install v0.1.5, which needs iOS 16 or later.

Pick the apps you open when idle

Put short videos, short dramas, games and social feeds on the list. Leave chat, maps and payment apps off.

Set the pause, open limits and daily limits

On Android turn on the pause before opening for those apps, then set a daily open limit and a daily limit. On iPhone add a schedule for the hours you scroll most when bored.

Hand over the unlock code

On Android turn on strict mode and Anti-Uninstall. Give the unlock code to a family member or friend, and keep the code for a child's phone yourself.

FAQ

I pick up my phone and scroll short videos whenever I am bored. How do I change that?

In Ripple on Android, turn on the pause before opening for your most opened short video app, then give it a daily open limit and a daily limit. Before it opens you see how many times you opened it today and how long you used it, and once the opens or minutes are used up it stays closed for the day.

My child asks for the phone every time they are bored. What can I do?

Agree on daily play time and give games and short videos a daily limit. For empty stretches like weekend afternoons add a schedule so those apps close. On Android turn on strict mode and Anti-Uninstall and keep the unlock code.

What is the pause before opening?

On Android, each time you open an app with this setting, you wait a few seconds first while the screen shows how many times you opened it today and how long you used it. Android only for now.

Does it work on iPhone?

iPhone v0.1.5 blocks selected apps by schedule, can block deleting apps, protects against clock changes and has an unlock code. It has no pause before opening, open limits or daily limits.

Will chat and map apps be locked?

Not if they are off the list. Only Complete Lock in a schedule leaves just the system essential apps.

Is it free? Do I need an account?

It is free with no ads and no account. See the FAQ for more.
