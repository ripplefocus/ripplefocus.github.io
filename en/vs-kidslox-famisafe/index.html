Ripple vs Kidslox and FamiSafe

Kidslox and FamiSafe are parental control apps. A parent installs them on the child's device and manages it from their own phone or a browser, with screen time limits, app blocking, location and different kinds of monitoring. Ripple is much smaller. It sets limits on the phone it is installed on, with no account, no parent app and no monitoring. I make Ripple, so read this with that in mind. Ripple for Android is v0.1.27 and Ripple for iPhone is v0.1.5. Facts about Kidslox and FamiSafe come from their websites and the US App Store listing in November 2026. Prices vary by country and change, so check before you pay.

What each one is

Kidslox

A parental control app for iPhone, iPad and Android. It has daily limits and schedules, blocking of single apps (on iOS it cuts internet access for a list of popular games and social apps), a web filter with SafeSearch and YouTube restricted mode, instant lock, location tracking, reports, social network and content monitoring, and protection so the child cannot remove it. One account covers up to 10 devices. The App Store listing says it has no ads, needs an internet connection, and gives a 3 day trial of Premium before switching to the free plan. Premium is a subscription, and a lifetime option exists.

FamiSafe

A parental control app by Wondershare for Android and iPhone. Its site lists AI detection of risky content in 30+ social apps, screen time limits and schedules, app rules, live location with geofencing, call log and message monitoring, viewing the child's screen, one way audio, YouTube and TikTok history, explicit image alerts and a driving report. It is sold as paid family plans.

Ripple

A free app with no ads, no account and no subscription. On Android (8.0 or later) it has schedules, a daily amount per app including 0 minutes, open limits, a break rule, quick focus, blocked websites, a usage calendar and strict mode. On iPhone v0.1.5 it only blocks by schedule, stops apps being deleted, protects the clock and uses an unlock code. It does not monitor messages, location or browsing.

For a child's phone

Managing from your own phone

Kidslox and FamiSafe let you change rules and lock the device remotely. Ripple cannot. Every rule is set on the child's phone, so you need the phone in your hand to change it.

Monitoring

If you want to see messages, location, watch history or alerts about risky content, Ripple does none of that. Kidslox or FamiSafe are the tools for it. Some families prefer limits without reading the child's messages, and that is where Ripple fits.

Per app limits

Ripple can give game, cartoon and short video apps their own daily amounts, set one game to 0 minutes so it stays closed all day, cap how many times an app opens, and add homework and bedtime schedules.

Rules the child cannot undo

Turn on strict mode and keep the code yourself. Anti-Uninstall turns on with strict mode and needs Android 11 or later. Changing the clock does not end a schedule. A forgotten code is lifted 72 hours after a request.

Child on iPhone or iPad

Ripple for iPhone v0.1.5 can block apps by schedule with a code you keep. It has no daily amounts. For daily limits on iPhone, Screen Time with Family Sharing is free and built in.

For your own phone

Limiting yourself

Kidslox and FamiSafe are built around a parent and a child. Ripple is made for the phone owner too: you set your own daily amounts, open limits and schedules on your own phone.

Making it hard to cheat

In Ripple, loosening a rule needs a wait of 15 minutes, or the unlock code. With strict mode only the code works, at most 3 successful uses a day. You can ask a partner or friend to set the code and keep it.

Privacy

Ripple needs no account and keeps its settings on the phone. It needs the Accessibility service on Android to see which app is open.

Short bursts of focus

Quick focus on Android locks distracting apps right away for a set time, for a meeting or an hour of study.

How to choose

Pick Kidslox

If you want to manage several family devices across iPhone and Android from one account, with web filtering and location, and you are fine with a subscription.

Pick FamiSafe

If monitoring social apps, messages and location matters most to you and you accept paying for a family plan.

Pick Ripple

If you want free per app limits and strict mode on an Android phone without an account or monitoring, for a child or for yourself.

Try it first

Install the latest APK, turn on the Accessibility service when asked, set a 30 minute daily amount on one app and open it to see the block screen. If your phone kills background apps, follow the keep alive guide.

FAQ

Can Ripple manage my child's phone from my phone?

No. Ripple has no parent app and no remote control. Rules are set on the child's phone and protected with strict mode and a code you keep.

Does Ripple read messages or track location?

No. It only knows which app is open so it can count time and show the block screen.

Is Ripple really free?

Yes. No ads, no subscription and no sign up. More in the FAQ.

What does the iPhone version do?

Ripple for iPhone v0.1.5 blocks apps by schedule, stops apps being deleted, protects the clock and uses an unlock code. It has no daily amounts.
