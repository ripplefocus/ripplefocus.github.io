Ripple vs FocusMe and BlockerX

All three try to make blocking hard to undo. FocusMe blocks apps and websites with plans you can make very strict, and it syncs with its computer apps. BlockerX is built mainly to filter adult content, with an accountability partner who hears about it if you uninstall. Ripple sets per app limits and schedules on the phone, with a wait or an unlock code before anything loosens. I make Ripple, so read this with that in mind. Ripple for Android is v0.1.21 and Ripple for iPhone is v0.1.5. Facts about FocusMe come from focusme.com and Google Play, and facts about BlockerX from Google Play and the US App Store, all in November 2026. Prices change and vary by country, so check before you pay.

What each one is

FocusMe

Apps for Android (8.0 or later) and iPhone (iOS 16 or later), free to download. Blocking, schedules and time limits work with no account. A plan can block apps and websites, set a time limit per day or hour, limit how many times a day something opens and how long each visit lasts, or run as a Quick Timer. Each plan has a strictness level, from one tap to stop up to Enforced, where it cannot be stopped until it ends. On Android, Tamper Protection with its own PIN keeps it from being switched off or uninstalled. Premium syncs plans with the Windows, Mac and Linux apps. A desktop licence is $17.95 a month, $119.40 a year or $449 once, with a 14 day free trial, and covers 2 phones or tablets.

BlockerX

Made by Atmana Tech for Android, iPhone, iPad and Mac. It filters adult websites with one switch, forces safe search and YouTube restricted mode, blocks words you list, and can block apps, websites, social media and games. An accountability partner gets told if you uninstall it, and the iPhone app can send them daily browsing reports. It has a community and articles. The Google Play version shows ads and in app purchases, and gambling blocking needs a subscription. The iPhone listing says a subscription is needed for all features, with Premium at $49.99 a month, $139.99 a year or $149.99 once in the US. Its Google Play data safety section says it may share personal info and device IDs with third parties.

Ripple

A free app with no ads, no account and no subscription. On Android (8.0 or later) it has schedules, a daily amount per app including 0 minutes, open limits, a break rule, quick focus, blocked websites, a usage calendar, a mode that closes only WeChat Channels and Moments, and strict mode. On iPhone v0.1.5 it only blocks by schedule, stops apps being deleted, protects the clock and uses an unlock code.

For your own phone

You keep turning the blocker off

FocusMe and Ripple both answer this. FocusMe makes you type random characters or blocks stopping altogether. In Ripple, loosening a rule or removing the app needs a 15 minute wait or the unlock code. Loosening a rule on apps like Douyin and Kuaishou waits 30 minutes. With strict mode only the code works, at most 3 successful uses a day, and a friend or partner can keep it for you.

Adult content

This is what BlockerX is for. Ripple has no adult content filter and no safe search. It only blocks websites you add to its list yourself, so it is a poor fit if this is your main worry.

Phone and laptop together

FocusMe can run the same plan on your computer and your phone. Ripple only runs on the phone.

Paying nothing

Ripple is free with nothing to unlock. FocusMe on the phone is free to start, and sync needs Premium. BlockerX keeps most features behind a subscription.

For a child's phone

A child who games or scrolls too long

Give each app its own daily amount in Ripple, or set a game to 0 minutes so it does not open all day. A homework schedule can start on its own every school day.

Rules the child cannot undo

Turn on strict mode in Ripple and keep the code yourself. Anti-Uninstall turns on with strict mode and needs Android 11 or later. Changing the clock does not end a schedule. A forgotten code is lifted 72 hours after a request. FocusMe Tamper Protection also uses a PIN you can keep.

Keeping teens away from adult sites

Ripple cannot filter adult content. BlockerX can, and the iPhone and iPad built in Screen Time has a free adult website filter too.

No remote management

Ripple has no parent app and sends nothing to anyone. You set rules on the child's phone and need it in your hand to change them. BlockerX tells a partner about an uninstall, which some families use as an alert.

Child on iPhone or iPad

Ripple for iPhone v0.1.5 can block apps by schedule with a code you keep. It has no daily amounts. FocusMe for iPhone has time limits, and Screen Time with Family Sharing is free and built in.

How to choose

Choose FocusMe

You want very strict plans, open and session limits on iPhone as well as Android, or the same plan on your computer.

Choose BlockerX

Your main goal is filtering adult content and you want a partner who gets told when you uninstall.

Choose Ripple

You want strict per app limits on Android for free, with no account and nothing sent off the phone, for yourself or for a child.

Try it first

Install the latest APK, turn on the accessibility service when asked, give one app 30 minutes a day and open it to see the block screen. If your phone closes apps in the background, follow the keep alive guide.

FAQ

Can Ripple block adult websites?

Only the ones you type into its blocked websites list. It has no filter, no category list and no safe search.

Does Ripple tell anyone if I remove it?

No. Ripple has no account and sends no reports. To remove it you wait first or enter the unlock code, and with Anti-Uninstall on the system blocks removal until then.

Is Ripple really free?

Yes. No ads, no subscription and no account. More in the FAQ.

What does the iPhone version do?

Ripple for iPhone v0.1.5 blocks apps by schedule, blocks deleting apps, protects the clock and uses an unlock code. It has no daily amounts.
