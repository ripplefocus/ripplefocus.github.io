Ripple vs Forest and OffPhone

Forest is one of the best known focus apps. Its site says it has more than 60 million downloads: a tree grows while you focus and dies if you leave for another app. OffPhone (远离手机) is a Chinese focus app that its site says has more than 10 million users on Android, iPhone and HarmonyOS, built around Pomodoro style phone locking. This page says where they do more than Ripple and who Ripple suits, first for adults limiting their own phone and then for parents. Ripple for Android is v0.1.20 and Ripple for iPhone is v0.1.5. Competitor details come from their websites in November 2026, so check their latest notes.

Adults limiting their own phone

What Forest does well

Each focus session grows a tree, and giving up kills it, which keeps a lot of people going. Deep Focus blocks other apps during a session and Time Guard blocks apps on a schedule. Forest's site says both are in the free plan, with free Time Guard limited to 1 app or site. Friends can grow one tree together, there is a browser extension, and your account syncs across iPhone, iPad, Android and the browser. Plus is a subscription priced by region and platform.

What OffPhone does well

It splits phone time into lock and break cycles, 25 minutes locked and 5 minutes off by default, and you can change both. You can give one app or a group of apps a time allowance within a period, after which they will not open. It has charts for lock time and app use, and an anti uninstall option. It runs on Android, iPhone and HarmonyOS. Check the app itself for its pricing.

Where Ripple for Android overlaps

Ripple for Android v0.1.20 also blocks by schedule, sets daily limits, locks the phone right away with Quick Focus, and has a usage calendar showing each day's time per app, open counts and block counts. All of it is free, with no ads, no in-app purchases and no account.

Where Ripple for Android differs

It can close only WeChat Channels and Moments while chat keeps working. It can cap how many times an app opens per day, or make you pause before an app opens. In strict mode, rules can only be loosened with an unlock code of at least 8 characters, used successfully at most 3 times a day, which you can give to a partner or friend. Anti-Uninstall turns on with it, and a forgotten code can be requested and is lifted after 72 hours. A 0 minute limit keeps an app closed all day.

What Ripple cannot do

There is no Pomodoro lock and break cycle, and no trees or group focus. There is no desktop app or browser extension. Ripple for iPhone v0.1.5 only blocks by schedule, blocks deleting apps, protects the clock and uses an unlock code, with no daily limits or usage stats. The APK comes from the website, so you will not find Ripple in app stores.

Parents setting up a child's phone

First: Ripple has no remote management

Ripple's settings live on that phone, so you set them up on the child's phone directly, and you cannot see or change them from your own phone. For that, use your phone maker's parental controls or Google Family Link on Android, and Family Sharing on iPhone.

The child keeps reaching for the phone during homework

Forest and OffPhone work well when the child is willing to tap start. When the child is not, Ripple can block on a schedule by itself, for example closing games and short video apps from 7 pm to 9 pm every day.

The child turns it off or uninstalls it

Turn on strict mode on the child's phone and keep the unlock code yourself. Anti-Uninstall turns on with it, and changing the phone clock does not help. To keep one game closed all day, give it 0 minutes.

You want to know how long the child played each day

The usage calendar in Ripple for Android v0.1.20 shows each day's time per app on the child's phone. Records are kept for 365 days and stay on that phone. To see them from your own phone you need a tool with remote management.

The child uses an iPhone or iPad

Ripple for iPhone can block by schedule on the child's device, for homework time and bedtime, with the unlock code kept by a parent. For daily limits or checking from your phone, iPhone Screen Time with Family Sharing covers more.

How to choose

You like Pomodoro and want to focus in blocks

Choose OffPhone. Lock and break cycles run on their own.

You need a bit of fun to keep going, or want to focus with friends

Choose Forest. Trees and group focus help a lot of people, and its browser extension covers your computer too.

You want some apps closed at fixed times every day, and rules you cannot casually switch off

Install Ripple for Android, turn on strict mode and give the unlock code to someone else. Ripple can also close only WeChat Channels.

You are setting rules on a child's Android phone without a subscription

Install Ripple on the child's phone and use schedules with strict mode. See also vs Opal and Freedom.

FAQ

Is Ripple a Forest alternative?

It depends on how you use Forest. If you want trees and timed focus sessions, stay with Forest, because Ripple has neither. If you want apps closed on a schedule or daily limits that are hard to undo, Ripple for Android does that for free.

Can I use Ripple and Forest together?

Yes. Some people time focus sessions with Forest and use Ripple to block short video apps and games on a schedule. Grant each app the permissions it asks for.

Can Ripple manage my child's phone remotely?

No. Ripple has no remote management. Set it up on the child's phone.

Does Ripple collect data?

Settings and usage stats stay on the phone and there is no account. Since v0.1.6 it counts once a day, anonymously, on first open, with only the platform, version, days since install, new install or update, app language and a random install ID made on the phone. No usage content is sent.

Does it cost anything?

Ripple is free, with no ads and no in-app purchases. More in the FAQ.
